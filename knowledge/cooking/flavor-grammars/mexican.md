# Mexican Flavor Grammar
Mexican flavor grammar repeats one move at every scale: toast or char an aromatic (dried/fresh chile, tomato, onion) to build smoke and depth, puree it into a sauce base, then "fry the puree" (freír la salsa) in hot fat to caramelize and concentrate before liquid goes in. This sear-the-puree step is the structural signature that separates Mexican cooking from cuisines that simmer raw aromatics. Lime is the dominant acid; smoke (chipotle) is a distinct axis from raw heat.

## Flavor Base / Aromatics
- [fact] Dried-chile "holy trinity" ancho + guajillo + pasilla covers different axes — ancho = raisin sweetness, guajillo = bright/tangy, pasilla = earthy/tobacco; mulato is a darker ancho relative #dried-chiles
- [fact] Fresh chiles by heat: poblano (mild, stuffed) < jalapeño < serrano (default raw-salsa chile) < habanero (fruity, searing) #fresh-chiles
- [fact] Toast dried chiles flat on a dry hot skillet ~15-30s/side until they "smell like coffee, not burnt," then soak in hot water 20-30 min and blend #toasting-chiles
- [pairing] Onion + garlic almost always cooked/charred (raw white onion in salsa is rinsed to tame bite); cumin used sparingly and toasted; achiote/annatto for color + earthy notes (recado rojo) #aromatics
- [fact] Mexican oregano (a verbena: citrusy, earthy) is botanically unrelated to Mediterranean oregano (a mint) and not freely interchangeable; epazote for beans #mexican-oregano

## Acid / Fat / Heat / Umami
- [pairing] Acid: lime (not lemon — the dominant fresh acid), tomatillo (built-in tartness for salsa verde), vinegar (escabeche, adobos), Yucatán sour orange #acid
- [pairing] Fat: lard/manteca (traditional, carries roast-pork flavor in beans/masa/moles), corn oil (modern neutral), avocado (fat as fresh ingredient) #fat
- [fact] Heat is a deliberate spectrum (~1,000 SHU ancho to ~200,000 habanero); smoke is a second axis supplied almost entirely by chipotle (smoke-dried jalapeño) #heat-smoke
- [pairing] Umami without dashi/soy: toasted dried chiles, charred tomato/tomatillo/onion (Maillard), tomato glutamate, dried shrimp (camarón seco), aged cheese (cotija, añejo) #umami

## Signature Combinations
- [pairing] Lime + cilantro + rinsed white onion — the raw finishing trinity of street tacos over rich grilled meat #taco-trinity
- [pairing] Tomatillo + serrano + cilantro (+ onion/garlic) = salsa verde; roast the tomatillos/chiles/garlic until blotchy, blend, fold in raw onion + cilantro #salsa-verde
- [pairing] Ancho + guajillo + cumin + vinegar + garlic = adobo; marinade for carne adobada, al pastor, birria #adobo
- [pairing] Chipotle + tomato — smoke + sweet acidity; backbone of tinga and chipotles in adobo #chipotle-tomato
- [pairing] Chocolate + chiles + warm spices + charred aromatics = mole, the maximal layered expression #mole

## Creative Application
Masa/nixtamalization (corn simmered in cal/calcium hydroxide) gives masa its cohesion (gelatinized starch, dissolved pericarp), the "tortilla" aroma, and a thickening role used in moles and stews. Mole is an architecture, not a recipe: chiles, toasted spices, toasted nuts/seeds (which also thicken), dried fruit, charred aromatics, and chocolate are each pureed separately and fried/reduced into the hot fat ONE AT A TIME, in order, so each caramelizes before the next — that is why a mole tastes "deep" not just "mixed." Riff A — "Guajillo-roasted carrot tacos": char carrots/onion, glaze in a fried guajillo+ancho+cumin adobo, finish with salsa verde, lime+onion+cilantro, pepitas, cotija. Riff B — "Chipotle-tomato mussels": steam in a fried chipotle+charred-tomato base, thicken broth with a spoon of masa, finish with lime + epazote.

## Pitfalls / Fixes
- Burnt/bitter dried chiles (over-toasting is worse than under-toasting): medium-low heat, watch constantly, pull at the "smells like coffee" point.
- Bitter sauce: remove seeds, don't over-soak; if anything scorched, discard soaking water and use fresh liquid; a pinch of sugar or lime rebalances.
- Raw/muddy sauce: always fry the blended puree in hot fat until it darkens and sizzles dry before adding liquid.
- Raw/pasty masa: cook tortillas through the three-flip puff sequence; steam tamales until masa pulls cleanly from the husk.
- Wrong oregano: use Mexican oregano with chiles, not Mediterranean.
