# Korean Flavor Grammar

Korean cooking thinks in 양념 (yangnyeom) — a seasoning logic built on fermented 장 (jang) pastes, raw aromatics, and nutty toasted sesame. Flavor is layered, savory-deep, and gently sweet-hot, with fermentation supplying the umami backbone rather than long stocks. The cook's identity lives in the personal ratio of garlic, sugar, gochugaru, soy, and sesame oil they whisk into a marinade.

## Flavor Base / Aromatics
- [fact] The seasoning system is called 양념 (yangnyeom); each cook keeps a personal ratio of the same pantry staples #yangnyeom
- [pairing] The working aromatic base is 마늘 (garlic) + 파 (scallion/green onion) + 생강 (ginger), almost always raw-minced into marinades and stews #aromatics
- [pairing] Three fermented 장 (jang) anchor savory depth: 간장 (ganjang, soy), 된장 (doenjang, soybean paste), 고추장 (gochujang, chili-soybean paste) — no true substitutes for authentic flavor #jang
- [fact] 고추가루 (gochugaru, sun-dried chili flakes) gives color and a fruity, low-acrid heat distinct from fresh chili #chili

## Acid / Fat / Heat / Umami
- [pairing] Acid: rice vinegar (식초) and Asian pear / fruit juice in marinades; raw garlic and 김치 brine add tang #acid
- [pairing] Fat: 참기름 (toasted sesame oil) as a fragrant finishing fat, plus 들기름 (perilla oil) and rendered pork/beef fat #fat
- [pairing] Heat: 고추가루 + 고추장 + fresh 청양고추 (cheongyang chili) — building, not searing #heat
- [pairing] Umami: doenjang, ganjang, 멸치 (anchovy) + 다시마 (kelp) stock, 새우젓 (salted shrimp), aged 김치 — fermentation-driven, stacked #umami
- [fact] Toasted sesame seeds + sesame oil are the signature nutty top-note across banchan and marinades #sesame

## Signature Combinations
- [pairing] 간장 + 마늘 + 설탕 + 참기름 + 깨 + 배즙 = the 불고기/갈비 sweet-soy marinade #marinade
- [pairing] 고추장 + 고추가루 + 간장 + 마늘 + 설탕 + 물엿 = 매콤달콤 (sweet-spicy) glaze for 제육볶음, 닭갈비, 떡볶이 #spicygochujang
- [pairing] 된장 + 마늘 + 멸치다시마 육수 = earthy savory base for 된장찌개 and 쌈장 #doenjang
- [pairing] 고추가루 + 액젓 + 마늘 + 생강 + 새우젓 = 김치 seasoning paste #kimchi
- [pairing] 참기름 + 소금 + 마늘 + 깨 = the universal 나물/무침 dress for vegetables #namul

## Creative Application
To compose a new Korean-feeling dish, pick a protein or vegetable, choose ONE jang as your savory spine, then balance it with the four levers: 마늘 (pungency), 설탕/물엿 (sweet), 고추가루 (heat/color), 참기름+깨 (nutty finish). Add a fermented umami booster (액젓 or 다시 stock) for depth. Example riffs: a gochujang-glazed roasted cauliflower finished with sesame and scallion (treats cauliflower like 닭갈비); a doenjang-butter pasta where doenjang replaces miso/anchovy for earthier umami. Keep aromatics raw or barely cooked to preserve the bright pungency.

## Pitfalls / Fixes
- Using Japanese miso for doenjang: doenjang is funkier and coarser — under-season and taste, it carries more salt and depth.
- Treating gochugaru and cayenne as interchangeable: gochugaru is fruity and mild; cayenne is hot and flat. Wrong heat curve.
- Skipping sugar/물엿: Korean savory-sweet balance collapses without a touch of sweetness against the salt and heat.
- Adding sesame oil early over high heat: it scorches and turns bitter — it is a finishing fat.
- Over-reducing into one-note salt: stack distinct umami sources instead of just more soy.
