# North African Flavor Grammar
North African (Moroccan-centric, with Tunisian/Algerian variation) cooking builds aromatic depth, not chili heat: warm spices bloomed into a slow onion-garlic-ginger base, layered with a sweet-savory axis that marries meat with dried fruit and honey. Preserved lemon is the central acid (and a source of fermented depth); olive oil and fermented butter (smen) are the fats. The conical tagine vessel self-bastes a braise with minimal liquid over low, steady heat — perfect for reducing fruit sugars into a glossy sauce without scorching.

## Flavor Base / Aromatics
- [fact] Ras el hanout ("top of the shop") has no fixed recipe — every shop/family blends its own, commonly a dozen to ~30 spices: cumin, coriander, cinnamon, ginger, cardamom, clove, nutmeg, mace, allspice, pepper, paprika, turmeric, plus floral notes (rosebuds, lavender) #ras-el-hanout
- [pairing] Warm-spice roles: cumin (earthy backbone), coriander (citrusy), cinnamon (the sweet-savory bridge), ginger, turmeric (color), paprika, saffron (festive, floral) #warm-spices
- [fact] Base technique: sweat onion in olive oil, then garlic + ginger + saffron until fragrant before meat and liquid; a heavily-onion sauce is itself named (daghmira, tfaya) #onion-base
- [pairing] Fresh cilantro + parsley = the twin herb base, bundled into the braise and scattered to finish; the herb core of chermoula #fresh-herbs

## Acid / Fat / Heat / Umami
- [pairing] Acid: preserved lemon (l'hamd markad — salt-cured ~30 days, salty-sour with floral/fermented depth) is central; fresh lemon and vinegar cut richness in salads #acid
- [pairing] Fat: olive oil (workhorse), smen (salted FERMENTED butter, blue-cheese funk, used sparingly for depth), argan oil (toasted, nutty finishing oil; amlou = argan + almond + honey) #fat
- [fact] Heat: harissa — Tunisian paste of roasted dried chiles + garlic + olive oil + the signature trio caraway + coriander + cumin (caraway's anise note is the hallmark); Tunisia builds it in, Morocco serves it milder/as a condiment #harissa
- [pairing] Umami/savory depth: preserved lemon, olives, smen (fermented funk), tomato, saffron (aromatic depth) — all four appear at once in chicken-with-preserved-lemon-and-olives #umami

## Signature Combinations
- [pairing] Lamb + apricot/prunes + ras el hanout — dried-fruit sweetness/acidity cuts fatty lamb; prunes thicken the sauce (mrouzia) #lamb-fruit
- [pairing] Chicken + preserved lemon + green olives (djaj mqualli) — the iconic tagine; salty-sour brightness + brine + ginger/saffron #chicken-preserved-lemon
- [pairing] Harissa + cumin + garlic — the Tunisian backbone for stews, couscous, marinades #harissa-cumin-garlic
- [pairing] Chermoula on fish — cilantro/parsley + garlic + cumin + paprika + lemon + oil; the defining traditional use, bright and penetrating #chermoula-fish
- [pairing] Honey + cinnamon + almond — the classic sweet trio (pastilla, briouats); orange-flower water often joins #honey-cinnamon-almond

## Creative Application
Chermoula = cilantro/parsley + garlic + cumin + paprika + lemon + olive oil (optional saffron, preserved lemon, cayenne); primarily a fish marinade, also a finishing sauce. The reusable stack: (1) olive oil + onion + garlic + ginger sweated low; (2) bloom warm spices/ras el hanout into the base (pre-bloom saffron in water); (3) braise protein/veg with minimal liquid, gentle; (4) optional sweet-savory axis — dried fruit + restrained honey + toasted almonds; (5) salty-sour-fresh finish — preserved lemon + olives in the last 15 min, fresh acid + herbs off heat, salt LAST. Riff A — "Roasted carrot + chickpea tagine with date and preserved lemon." Riff B — "Chermoula-roasted salmon with herbed couscous," finished with argan oil.

## Pitfalls / Fixes
- Stale/raw spices: buy whole, grind fresh, bloom in warm fat ~30-60s after the onion to kill the dusty taste.
- Saffron: dry-roast threads lightly, grind, steep in hot (not boiling) liquid; turmeric is a COLOR substitute only, never a flavor substitute.
- Preserved lemon: always rinse; rind adds perfumed funk, pulp adds salty-sour body (use of pulp varies by cook).
- Over-salting: preserved lemon + olives are already salty — salt last.
- Over-sweetening sweet-savory tagines: let dried fruit carry sweetness, use honey sparingly, rebalance with acid + salt + fresh herbs at the end.
- Couscous: authentic couscous is STEAMED (raked/fluffed between passes), not just hydrated with boiling water.
