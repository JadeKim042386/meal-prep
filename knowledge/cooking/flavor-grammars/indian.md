# Indian Flavor Grammar

Indian cooking thinks in spice layering and timing: the same masala behaves differently depending on WHEN it enters the pan. Whole spices bloom first in hot fat, ground spices cook into the base, and finishing aromatics like garam masala go in last to keep their volatile perfume. The 따드카 (tadka / tempering) — spices sizzled in ghee or oil — is the technique that converts dry powder into deep aroma, and it works both as a foundation and as a finishing flourish.

## Flavor Base / Aromatics
- [pairing] The northern aromatic base is the 'wet masala': onion + ginger + garlic (often + tomato + green chili) cooked down (bhuna) until jammy #basemasala
- [pairing] 따드카 (tadka / tempering, aka chaunk, baghar, phodni): whole spices — cumin, mustard seed, dried chili, curry leaf, asafoetida (hing) — bloomed in hot ghee/oil #tadka
- [fact] Layering order matters: whole spices first (need heat + time), ground spices next (bloom briefly before liquid), finishing spices like garam masala last #layering
- [pairing] North leans warming spices + dairy (cream, yogurt, ghee, paneer); South leans mustard seed + curry leaf + coconut + tamarind + rice, with more tempering #regional

## Acid / Fat / Heat / Umami
- [pairing] Acid: tamarind (imli), lime, yogurt (dahi), tomato, amchur (dried mango), kokum — region-dependent #acid
- [pairing] Fat: ghee (clarified butter) for richness and aroma, plus mustard oil (East/Bengal), coconut oil (South), neutral oils — fat is the carrier that blooms spice #fat
- [pairing] Heat: green chili (fresh), dried red chili, cayenne/Kashmiri chili (color + mild), black pepper; balanced against dairy/coconut to cool #heat
- [pairing] Umami: onion-tomato base, fermented elements (dosa/idli batter, dahi), asafoetida, browned (bhuna) aromatics, dried fenugreek (kasuri methi) #umami
- [fact] Garam masala is a finishing blend (warm spices: cinnamon, clove, cardamom, cumin, coriander, pepper) — added late, not cooked long #garammasala

## Signature Combinations
- [pairing] cumin + coriander + turmeric + chili + onion-tomato base = the everyday North Indian curry masala #currybase
- [pairing] mustard seed + curry leaf + dried chili + asafoetida in hot oil = South Indian tadka for dal, sambar, vegetables #southtadka
- [pairing] ginger + garlic paste + yogurt + garam masala + Kashmiri chili = tandoori / tikka marinade #tikka
- [pairing] cumin + black cardamom + cinnamon + clove + bay = the warm whole-spice layer for biryani / pulao and rich gravies #biryanispice
- [pairing] kasuri methi (dried fenugreek) + cream + tomato + butter = the makhani / butter-gravy signature #makhani

## Creative Application
Compose an Indian-feeling dish by thinking in three time-slots: (1) bloom whole spices in ghee/oil, (2) build and brown a base — onion-ginger-garlic-tomato in the north, or just the tempered tadka in the south — and add ground spices to cook off raw edges, (3) finish with garam masala, fresh herbs (cilantro), a squeeze of acid, and/or a swirl of cream or yogurt. Choose a region to keep coherent: north = dairy + warm spice; south = mustard/curry-leaf + coconut/tamarind. Example riffs: a chickpea-and-squash 'sabzi' built on cumin-tadka + turmeric + amchur; a paneer-or-tofu makhani finished with kasuri methi and cream. Let the fat carry the spice — never add powdered spice to a dry, cool pan.

## Pitfalls / Fixes
- Adding ground spices to dry/cool oil: they scorch and taste raw or burnt — bloom briefly in fat, or after the onions.
- Under-browning the base (bhuna): a pale, watery onion base tastes thin; cook it down deeply for sweetness and depth.
- Cooking garam masala long: its volatile aromatics boil off — add it near the end.
- Burning whole spices in the tadka: they go from fragrant to bitter in seconds; pull from heat the moment cumin/mustard pops.
- Treating 'curry powder' as a stand-in for layered masala: pre-mixed powder added all at once flattens the dish's structure.
