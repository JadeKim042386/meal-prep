# Spanish Flavor Grammar
Spanish cooking is built on smoke and savor, not heat: a slow-cooked sofrito of onion, garlic, and tomato in olive oil at the front, and a picada (ground nuts, bread, garlic) or alioli at the back. Pimentón (oak-smoked paprika) and saffron set the color and aroma; sherry vinegar (vinagre de Jerez) is the rounded, nutty acid; jamón, saffron, and tomato stack umami. Garlic appears at every layer — raw, slow-cooked, and emulsified.

## Flavor Base / Aromatics
- [fact] Sofrito = onion + garlic + tomato (sometimes green pepper) cooked low and slow in olive oil until jammy and concentrated; the base for paella, stews, rice dishes (Roden: "Spain's signature tune") #sofrito
- [fact] Garlic is structurally central — in sofrito, alioli, picada, rubbed raw onto pan con tomate, the star of gambas al ajillo #garlic
- [fact] Pimentón = paprika from peppers dried over holm-oak fire (Pimentón de la Vera, Extremadura); grades dulce (sweet, everyday), agridulce (bittersweet), picante (hot) #pimenton
- [pairing] Restrained herb palette: bay leaf (laurel) in braises, parsley (perejil) fresh at the finish and in picada #herbs

## Acid / Fat / Heat / Umami
- [pairing] Acid: sherry vinegar (vinagre de Jerez, solera-aged, nutty, LOWER acidity than wine vinegar — adds roundness not just sourness) is central; wine and lemon secondary #acid
- [pairing] Fat: olive oil (central — sofrito, alioli, pan con tomate, frying, finishing), jamón fat for depth, lard in some regions #fat
- [fact] Heat is mild: pimentón picante and guindilla peppers (~1,000-2,000 SHU, milder than jalapeño); Spanish food is smoke-and-savor, not capsaicin #heat
- [pairing] Umami stacks glutamate: jamón ibérico/serrano (free tyrosine from long curing), saffron, pimentón, tomato/tomato paste, manchego, anchovy/bonito, and sofrito itself #umami

## Signature Combinations
- [pairing] Garlic + olive oil + pimentón — the smoky-savory base trio (gambas al ajillo, patatas); bloom pimentón briefly in warm garlicky oil off high heat #garlic-oil-pimenton
- [pairing] Jamón + almost anything — a few grams add salt, umami, fat depth to eggs, croquetas, beans, melon #jamon
- [pairing] Saffron + bomba rice + sofrito — the paella axis; sofrito base, saffron-infused stock, bomba carrier #paella-axis
- [pairing] Sherry vinegar + olive oil — the canonical Spanish dressing; nutty low-acid vinegar against fruity oil #sherry-vinaigrette
- [pairing] Tomato + garlic + olive oil = pan con tomate — toasted bread rubbed with raw garlic and fresh tomato, dressed with oil and salt #pan-con-tomate

## Creative Application
Saffron supplies color (crocin) and honey-floral aroma (safranal): bloom threads in warm (not boiling) stock 10-20 min, add early. Bomba/Calasparra rice absorbs 3-4x its volume in stock while staying separate — a flavor carrier. Picada is the Catalan back-end finisher/thickener: pounded toasted nuts (almonds, hazelnuts) + garlic + fried bread + parsley, stirred in the last 2-3 min to thicken, enrich, and add raw-garlic lift. Alioli is authentically egg-free (garlic + oil + salt emulsified by garlic's own surfactants). The grammar: sofrito front-loads depth → starch/protein carrier → saffron or pimentón sets the color/smoke axis → sherry vinegar acid + a picada or alioli finisher. Riff A — "Arroz de calabaza y avellana": sofrito + bloomed pimentón dulce + bomba + saffron stock, finished with a hazelnut-garlic-parsley picada. Riff B — "Setas a la Jerezana": mushrooms seared with garlic + guindilla, deglazed with fino sherry then sherry vinegar, finished with alioli.

## Pitfalls / Fixes
- Burning pimentón → bitter: it scorches at ~80°C (high sugar); pull the pan off heat, let oil cool, then bloom ~20s, or mix into cold oil, or add with the liquid.
- Rushing the sofrito: low heat and patience (often 30-45+ min) — sweating sweet and jammy, not browning, is the single biggest lever on final flavor.
- Breaking the alioli: pound garlic with salt to a paste first; add oil drop-by-drop at the start, speed up only once stable.
- Adding picada too early: loosen with a spoon of braising liquid and stir in only the final 2-3 min or it loses its fresh lift.
- Wrong vinegar: use vinagre de Jerez (lower acid, nutty), added off heat — harsh wine vinegar makes a dish sharp instead of round.
- Expecting heat: it's a smoke-and-savor cuisine; keep heat to a whisper.
