# American BBQ Flavor Grammar
American barbecue is built on one primary flavor — wood smoke — laid onto the surface of tough, collagen-rich meat cooked low and slow until collagen melts to gelatin. A dry rub builds a Maillard-and-smoke crust (the bark); regional sauces then play the sweet / tangy / spicy / smoky / umami axes in different proportions. The flavor lives in the bark and the aroma; the smoke ring is cosmetic and adds no taste.

## Flavor Base / Aromatics
- [fact] Smoke flavor comes from trace phenols — syringol (aroma) and guaiacol (taste) — released by clean combustion (~650-750°F, plenty of oxygen) producing thin blue smoke; thick white/gray smoke = bitter creosote #smoke
- [fact] Wood intensity mild→strong: apple/cherry < pecan < oak < hickory < mesquite; oak (post oak) is the Texas beef standard, hickory the classic pork wood, fruit woods mild/sweet #woods
- [fact] Low and slow (~225-275°F): collagen converts to gelatin (silky, moisture-holding) starting ~160°F over time; brisket/shoulder pulled at ~195-203°F, far past doneness #low-and-slow
- [fact] The bark = Maillard browning + a polymerized protein skin (pellicle) + the dry rub crust; sugar caramelization is NOT the main driver (sugar needs ~300°F, above smoker temps) #bark

## Acid / Fat / Heat / Umami
- [pairing] Dry rub roles: salt (penetrates deep, retains moisture, builds bark), brown sugar (Maillard, butterscotch), paprika (color), black pepper (heat + bark), garlic/onion powder (umami), cayenne (heat), mustard powder (tang), cumin (warmth) #dry-rub
- [fact] Dry-brine: salt separately and ahead (it penetrates while the rest of the rub stays on the surface); ~½ tsp kosher salt per pound, hours to overnight; denatures proteins for juicier meat #dry-brine
- [fact] Central Texas "Dalmatian rub" = coarse salt + coarse black pepper ~50/50, no sugar; beef rubs omit sugar so beef + post-oak smoke dominate #dalmatian-rub
- [pairing] The five axes: sweet (sugar/molasses/honey), tangy (vinegar/mustard), spicy (cayenne/pepper), smoky (wood), umami (the meat, Maillard bark, tomato, Worcestershire/anchovy, MSG) #flavor-axes

## Signature Combinations
- [pairing] Salt + pepper + beef + post oak = Central Texas brisket — minimal rub, beef and smoke dominate #texas-brisket
- [pairing] Brown sugar + paprika + pork + hickory = classic Memphis-style ribs/shoulder (Memphis Dust) #memphis-pork
- [pairing] Vinegar + red pepper + pork = Eastern NC whole-hog (no tomato); acid cuts the fatty pork #eastern-nc
- [pairing] Yellow mustard + vinegar + sugar = South Carolina "Carolina Gold" mustard sauce on pulled pork #carolina-gold
- [pairing] Molasses + tomato (ketchup) + sweet = Kansas City — thick sweet sauce, the national default #kansas-city

## Creative Application
Regional sauce logic spans sweet/thick (KC: tomato + molasses) → tangy (Memphis: tomato-vinegar) → austere vinegar-pepper (Eastern NC) → vinegar-tomato (Lexington/Western NC) → mustard (SC Carolina Gold) → beef-minimal salt-pepper-smoke (Texas) → mayo outlier (Alabama white sauce, for smoked chicken). The grammar: pick a protein + matching wood intensity, lock a salt foundation (dry brine), choose a regional axis emphasis, build bark via Maillard, sauce LATE if at all. Riff A — "Carolina Gold smoked salmon collar": light dry brine, mild fruit wood so smoke doesn't bully the fish, mustard-vinegar-honey glaze in the final minutes (acid cuts the fat). Riff B — "Texas-style smoked king oyster mushrooms": Dalmatian rub + a Worcestershire/soy + MSG brush to engineer in the umami the meat would supply, post-oak smoke, low and slow to bark.

## Pitfalls / Fixes
- Too much smoke / creosote: keep a small hot fire with visible flame, vents open, control temp by adjusting fuel not by choking airflow — aim for thin blue smoke. Do NOT soak wood (it cools the fire and makes dirty smoke).
- Not enough salt: dry brine ahead — ~½ tsp kosher salt per pound (kosher is ~half as salty by volume as table salt); salt well in advance, ideally overnight for thick cuts.
- Sauce burning: sauces with sugar scorch under heat — sauce only the last ~15-30 min and/or over indirect heat; build bark with rub first.
- Drying out lean cuts: chicken/pork loin overcook fast (no collagen cushion) — pull at precise lower targets; cook fatty/collagen-rich cuts long to ~203°F.
- The stall (temp plateaus ~150-170°F): it's evaporative cooling, not fat/collagen rendering — wrap in foil/butcher paper at ~150-160°F (the Texas Crutch) or cook hotter to push through.
