# Japanese Flavor Grammar

Japanese cooking thinks from umami outward: 다시 (dashi) is the foundation, and seasonings are added in restraint to enhance rather than mask an ingredient's natural flavor. The grammar is subtractive — balance sweet, salty, and sour so no single taste dominates, and let the primary ingredient speak. A small fixed vocabulary (소유, 미린, 사케, 미소, 사토) recombines in clean ratios.

## Flavor Base / Aromatics
- [fact] The flavor foundation is 다시 (dashi): umami extracted from 다시마 (kombu, glutamate) + 가쓰오부시 (katsuobushi/bonito, inosinate) — a textbook glutamate-inosinate synergy #dashi
- [pairing] Aromatics are restrained and clean: 생강 (ginger), 파 (negi scallion), 미쓰바, 와사비, shiso, yuzu zest — used as bright accents, not a sautéed base #aromatics
- [fact] The seasoning order 사-시-스-세-소 (sa-shi-su-se-so) = sugar, salt, vinegar, soy, miso — add sugar before soy so salt doesn't block sweetness; soy/miso last to preserve aroma #sashisuseso
- [fact] 미린 (mirin) supplies gentle fermented sweetness, gloss (luster), and masks fishy/gamy smells #mirin

## Acid / Fat / Heat / Umami
- [pairing] Acid: 쌀식초 (rice vinegar) and citrus — yuzu, sudachi, ponzu — light and aromatic rather than sharp #acid
- [pairing] Fat: low overall; neutral oils for tempura, 참기름 only as a Chinese/Korean-leaning accent — fat is minimized to keep clarity #fat
- [pairing] Heat: minimal — 와사비, karashi mustard, shichimi togarashi, yuzu kosho as condiment heat, not built into the dish #heat
- [pairing] Umami: stacked and deep — dashi + 소유 (shoyu) + 미소 (miso) + dried/fermented goods (kombu, niboshi, katsuobushi) #umami
- [fact] The golden ratio dashi : soy : mirin = 8 : 1 : 1 gives the balanced base for noodle broths, simmered dishes #goldenratio

## Signature Combinations
- [pairing] 다시 + 소유 + 미린 (8:1:1) = all-purpose simmering/노odle base (煮物, うどん dashi) #dashibase
- [pairing] 소유 + 미린 + 사케 + 설탕 = 테리야키 / 야키토리 tare glaze #teriyaki
- [pairing] 미소 + 다시 = 미소시루 (miso soup) and 미소-marinades (saikyo-style with sweet white miso) #miso
- [pairing] 쌀식초 + 설탕 + 소금 = 스시 rice seasoning (sushi-zu) #sushizu
- [pairing] 폰즈 = citrus + 소유 + dashi/kombu = bright dipping/dressing acid #ponzu

## Creative Application
Build a new Japanese-feeling dish by starting with a dashi or kombu base for umami, then season minimally toward an 8:1:1-ish balance of umami:salt:sweet, and finish with one bright accent (yuzu, shiso, grated ginger). Let one ingredient be the star. Example riffs: roasted mushrooms glazed in mirin-soy-dashi then finished with yuzu zest (a 照り焼き logic on vegetables); a saikyo-miso marinade on cod-substitute or eggplant for sweet-savory depth. Restraint is the move — stop seasoning earlier than instinct says.

## Pitfalls / Fixes
- Drowning the dish in soy: shoyu is a seasoning, not the flavor — dashi should lead, soy should support.
- Skipping dashi and relying on soy + sugar: you get salty-sweet but no umami round, the broth tastes thin.
- Boiling kombu hard: it turns slimy and bitter — steep gently, remove before a rolling boil.
- Adding miso then boiling: high heat kills miso's aroma — add off-heat at the end (same for soy in delicate dishes).
- Wrong seasoning order: sugar after soy stays muted; follow sa-shi-su-se-so.
