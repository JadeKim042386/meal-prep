# French Flavor Grammar
French cuisine builds flavor through controlled depth: an aromatic vegetable base sweated in fat, a long-extracted stock (fond), and a sauce that ties the plate together. Its signature move is the protein + sauce + garniture + starch structure, with acid (wine, vinegar, lemon) used as the lever that lightens richness. Heat is pungency (pepper), never chili. Mastery is about emulsion, reduction, and seasoning balance rather than bold spicing.

## Flavor Base / Aromatics
- [fact] Mirepoix = 2:1:1 by weight onion:carrot:celery, diced and sweated slowly in butter without browning; the base for stocks, soups, stews, sauces #mirepoix
- [fact] White mirepoix omits carrot (parsnip + leek substitute) to keep fond blanc and cream sauces pale #white-mirepoix
- [fact] Bouquet garni = thyme + bay leaf + parsley stems tied/wrapped, simmered then removed; sachet d'épices holds dry spices/peppercorns #bouquet-garni
- [fact] Fines herbes = parsley, chives, tarragon, chervil, finely chopped fresh and added at the end (heat destroys their aroma); codified by Escoffier 1903 #fines-herbes
- [pairing] Shallot = milder, sweeter than onion, favored for sauces, vinaigrettes, finishing; garlic used sparingly and added late (burns fast) #shallot
- [fact] "Sweating" softens aromatics in butter without color; butter as a fat carries fat-soluble aroma compounds through the dish #sweating

## Acid / Fat / Heat / Umami
- [pairing] Acid: wine (red with meat, white with poultry/fish), vinegar (red wine, sherry, champagne), lemon, lactic tang of crème fraîche; deglaze the fond and reduce au sec #acid
- [pairing] Fat: butter dominates (beurre noisette = browned, nutty), duck/goose fat in the southwest (confit), cream/crème fraîche, lardons #fat
- [fact] Heat is pepper, not chili: white pepper for light sauces/fish, mignonette (coarse-crushed peppercorns) for steak au poivre #pepper
- [pairing] Umami: stocks/fonds and demi-glace are the backbone; glace de viande (deeply reduced stock), anchovy, aged cheese (Gruyère, Parmesan), mushrooms (cèpes, morels), tomato concentrate #umami
- [fact] Demi-glace = sauce espagnole + brown veal stock, reduced by half; roasting bones first (fond brun) adds Maillard depth #demi-glace

## Signature Combinations
- [pairing] Butter + shallot + white wine — the universal bistro pan sauce; reduce, then monter au beurre off-heat for gloss #pan-sauce
- [pairing] Beurre blanc — shallots reduced in white wine + vinegar, cold butter whisked in; acid suspends milk solids so it's creamy not oily (canonical with fish) #beurre-blanc
- [pairing] Cream + tarragon — poulet à l'estragon, béarnaise; tarragon's anise (estragole) lifts butter/egg/cream richness #tarragon
- [pairing] Persillade (garlic + parsley + butter) — escargots, crusts; add lemon zest, drop the fat → gremolata #persillade
- [pairing] Red wine + lardons + mushroom + onion — boeuf bourguignon / coq au vin; wine tenderizes, smoke + earth + sweet layer umami #bourguignonne

## Creative Application
Compose: protein + a mother sauce (or daughter derivative matched to the protein) + a named garniture + starch, with acid as the brightening lever. Take a mother sauce and add one defining ingredient: béchamel + Gruyère = Mornay; velouté + cream + lemon = suprême; espagnole + red wine + shallot + marrow = bordelaise; hollandaise + tarragon-shallot reduction = béarnaise. Riff: "Poulet à l'estragon" — sear chicken, build a chicken velouté/suprême, deglaze with white wine + shallot, finish with cream + tarragon + Dijon, monter au beurre. Or "Saint-Jacques, beurre blanc au cidre" — beurre blanc with dry cider + Calvados replacing some wine, importing the Normandy axis into the canonical structure.

## Pitfalls / Fixes
- Broken hollandaise/béarnaise (heat too high, butter added too fast): whisk in 1 Tbsp cold water if too hot, or restart by whisking the broken sauce into a fresh yolk.
- Broken beurre blanc (over 50-65°C, under-reduced base): off heat, whisk in a cold butter cube or ice to re-solidify.
- Over-reduction (too salty/gluey): dilute with unsalted stock/water/cream; add acid to cut salt.
- Roux lumps: pair opposite temperatures — hot liquid into cooled roux, or cold into hot.
- Monter au beurre: swirl cold butter into hot-but-not-boiling sauce (~80°C) off heat for gloss; too hot breaks it.
