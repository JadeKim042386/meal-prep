# Italian Flavor Grammar
Italian cooking prizes restraint: few, perfect ingredients allowed to speak for themselves, built on a slow-sweated aromatic base (soffritto). Its central axis is regional — butter/rice/cream in the North versus olive oil/tomato/chile/dried pasta in the South — and its philosophy is cucina povera (nothing wasted, humble ingredients elevated by technique). Acid (tomato, vinegar, lemon) cuts richness; umami comes from aged cheese, cured pork, anchovy, and tomato.

## Flavor Base / Aromatics
- [fact] Soffritto = battuto (minced onion/carrot/celery, ~2:1:1 onion-dominant, sometimes with cured pork fat) sweated slowly in fat until soft and golden, never browned; the base for sauces, soups, ragù, risotto #soffritto
- [fact] Restraint is the governing principle (Marcella Hazan: "what you keep out is just as important as what you put in") — exemplar is the 3-ingredient tomato-butter-onion sauce #restraint
- [pairing] Garlic-vs-onion tendency: North leans onion (with butter), South leans garlic (with olive oil) — a tendency, not a rule (garlic is in fact ubiquitous; amatriciana traditionally has no garlic) #garlic-onion
- [pairing] Basil (fresh, off-heat, southern, tomato partner), oregano (dried, pizza herb), rosemary (Tuscan, woody, with roasts), sage (northern, with butter) #herbs

## Acid / Fat / Heat / Umami
- [pairing] Acid cuts richness: tomato (cooked acidity + glutamate), balsamic (Modena/Reggio PDO, northern), lemon (southern, seafood, gremolata), wine/red wine vinegar for deglazing #acid
- [pairing] Fat is the North/South axis: butter/cream north (drives risotto mantecatura), extra-virgin olive oil south (Liguria is the northern oil exception) #fat
- [fact] Cured pork fats: guanciale = cured jowl, fattier, the traditional carbonara/amatriciana/gricia fat; pancetta = cured belly; lardo = back fat #cured-pork
- [fact] Heat = peperoncino, predominantly Calabrian; 'nduja is the spreadable spicy Calabrian salami; used as background warmth #peperoncino
- [pairing] Umami: Parmigiano-Reggiano (free glutamate rises with aging), tomato/tomato paste, anchovy (melts into sauces as seasoning, not fishiness), cured meats, dried porcini (nucleotide-rich — porcini + Parmesan = synergy powerhouse) #umami

## Signature Combinations
- [pairing] Tomato + basil + mozzarella — Caprese, Pizza Margherita; basil's green volatiles, tomato's acid+glutamate, mozzarella's fat buffer the acid (no vinegar) #caprese
- [pairing] Garlic + olive oil + peperoncino = aglio e olio; cook garlic to just golden (never burn), emulsify with starchy pasta water #aglio-e-olio
- [pairing] Roman trio guanciale + Pecorino Romano + black pepper: gricia (just that), + cheese/pepper alone = cacio e pepe, + egg = carbonara (no cream), + tomato = amatriciana #roman-pastas
- [pairing] Sage + butter (burro e salvia) — northern sauce for gnocchi/ravioli; brown the butter, crisp the sage, emulsify with pasta water #sage-butter
- [pairing] Anchovy + garlic + olive oil — bagna cauda, puttanesca base; gentle heat melts anchovy into smooth umami #anchovy-garlic

## Creative Application
The grammar: pick a fat, a single aromatic lead, one starch/vegetable hero, one umami source, an acid or herb to brighten — and stop. Borrow an existing skeleton and swap one variable. Riff A — "Gricia di mare": keep the gricia structure (rendered fat + pecorino + pepper + pasta-water emulsion) but swap guanciale for bottarga as the salty umami carrier, with lemon zest to brighten. Riff B — "Risotto ai porcini e salvia": northern skeleton (butter + onion soffritto, Carnaroli, white wine, mantecatura with butter + Parmigiano), layering dried porcini (nucleotides) with Parmigiano (glutamate) for deliberate umami synergy, finished with sage in brown butter. If a dish has more than ~5-6 components, cut until each does distinct work.

## Pitfalls / Fixes
- Over-saucing: finish drained al dente pasta IN the pan of sauce, tossing with pasta water; sauce is a condiment, not a pool.
- Burning garlic: add to cold oil, warm slowly over medium-low, pull at light golden — burnt garlic turns bitter and ruins the dish.
- Not using pasta water: reserve ~1 cup; the starch is what emulsifies fat and cheese into a clinging sauce (mantecatura).
- Salting pasta water: aim ~1% (≈1½ tsp fine salt per liter), not "salty like the sea" (3.5% is inedible).
- Broken cheese/egg emulsions (cacio e pepe, carbonara): work off intense heat (~160°F); proteins seize/scramble above ~200°F.
