# Vietnamese Flavor Grammar

Vietnamese cooking thinks in balance and brightness: 느억맘 (nước mắm, fish sauce) is the savory soul, but it is always tuned against lime, sugar, and chili and lifted by mountains of fresh raw herbs. Where Thai food pounds aromatics into pastes, Vietnamese food keeps herbs whole and raw at the table — the diner finishes the dish. The grammar is light, clean, and aromatic, with restrained spicing.

## Flavor Base / Aromatics
- [fact] 느억맘 (nước mắm, fermented fish sauce of fish + salt) is the foundational salt + umami of the cuisine #fishsauce
- [pairing] Cooking aromatics: shallot + garlic + ginger + lemongrass (sả) + scallion, often as a quick stir-fry base or in marinades #aromatics
- [pairing] Fresh raw herbs are eaten as a vegetable, not a garnish: cilantro, mint, Thai basil, rau răm (Vietnamese coriander), perilla (tía tô), dill (thì là, esp. with turmeric) #herbs
- [fact] The defining table condiment is 느억짬 (nước chấm): fish sauce balanced with lime, sugar, water, garlic, chili #nuoccham

## Acid / Fat / Heat / Umami
- [pairing] Acid: chanh (lime), rice vinegar, and pickled vegetables (đồ chua) for brightness and contrast #acid
- [pairing] Fat: light — neutral oil, rendered shallot/garlic oil (mỡ hành), coconut milk in the south, pork fat in broths #fat
- [pairing] Heat: fresh bird's-eye chili and chili sauces added at the table, not built deep into the dish — gentle by default #heat
- [pairing] Umami: nước mắm + slow-simmered pork/beef bone broths (phở) + dried shrimp + caramel (nước màu/nước hàng) for savory-bitter depth #umami
- [fact] Caramel sauce (nước màu) — sugar cooked dark — gives kho braises their bittersweet mahogany savor #caramel

## Signature Combinations
- [pairing] 느억맘 + lime + sugar + water + garlic + chili = 느억짬, the dipping/dressing sauce that touches nearly everything #nuoccham
- [pairing] nước mắm + caramel (nước màu) + shallot + black pepper = kho braise (thịt kho, cá kho) #kho
- [pairing] lemongrass + garlic + shallot + nước mắm + sugar = grilled-meat marinade (thịt nướng, bún chả) #lemongrassmarinade
- [pairing] herbs + lettuce + rice noodle + protein + nước chấm = the deconstructed 'assemble at table' bowl/roll logic (bún, gỏi cuốn) #assembly
- [pairing] bone broth + charred ginger + onion + star anise + cinnamon + fish sauce = phở aromatic spine #pho

## Creative Application
Build a Vietnamese-feeling dish by setting a balanced nước chấm or nước mắm seasoning, keeping the cooked part simple and light, then layering big handfuls of raw herbs and a crunchy-fresh element (pickles, lettuce, bean sprouts) so the diner assembles brightness themselves. Example riffs: a lemongrass-marinated roasted mushroom 'bún' bowl with vermicelli, raw herbs, pickled carrot, and nước chấm; a tofu kho braised in caramel + fish sauce + black pepper. Think contrast: warm-cool, soft-crunchy, rich-bright in the same bite.

## Pitfalls / Fixes
- Skimping on fresh herbs: in Vietnamese food herbs are a vegetable portion, not a sprinkle — be generous and keep them raw.
- Unbalanced nước chấm: straight fish sauce is too salty/funky; it must be cut with lime, sugar, and water to taste.
- Over-spicing / heavy spice paste: Vietnamese food is restrained — let freshness and balance lead, add chili at the table.
- Substituting dried herbs (esp. dill, rau răm): the aromatics are about raw volatile freshness; dried versions fall flat.
- Forgetting acid + crunch contrast: pickles and lime are structural, not optional garnish.
