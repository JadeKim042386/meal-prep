# Salt Fat Acid Heat

Samin Nosrat's framework reduces good cooking to mastering four variables: **Salt** (enhances flavor), **Fat** (carries flavor and builds texture), **Acid** (balances and brightens), and **Heat** (determines texture and browning). Treating them as the "cardinal directions" of cooking turns recipes from instructions into adjustable levers — once you can read and steer all four, you can invent or rescue almost any dish without a recipe.

## Principle / Mechanism
- [principle] Salt enhances and unifies flavor; it suppresses bitterness and amplifies perceived sweetness, acidity, and aroma rather than just tasting "salty" #salt
- [principle] Fat is the third macronutrient with flavor, a flavor carrier (many aroma compounds are fat-soluble), and a texture generator (crisp, creamy, flaky, tender) #fat
- [principle] Acid balances richness and provides contrast; it raises perceived brightness and stimulates salivation, making food more "mouthwatering" #acid
- [principle] Heat governs physical and chemical transformation — protein coagulation, gelatinization, Maillard browning, caramelization, evaporation — and thus final texture #heat
- [fact] The four are independent dials: a flat dish is usually missing salt OR acid, a heavy dish needs acid, a dull dish needs heat-driven browning #diagnosis

## Why It Works
The four elements map onto distinct sensory and physical channels, so adjusting one rarely cancels another. Salt and acid act on taste receptors and also modulate aroma release, which is why a pinch of salt or a squeeze of lemon can make a dish "pop" without adding new ingredients (Harold McGee notes salt and acid in particular bring out all other flavor components). Fat coats the tongue and dissolves volatile aroma molecules, prolonging and spreading flavor. Heat is the only element that irreversibly restructures the food — denaturing proteins, browning surfaces, driving off water — which is why it sets texture. Because each lever operates through a different mechanism, a cook who diagnoses which channel is weak can fix a dish with a single targeted move.

## Creative Application
Use the four as a diagnostic checklist when tasting: "Does it need salt? Acid? Is it rich enough (fat)? Did it brown enough (heat)?" To invent a dish, pick a protein/vegetable, then deliberately assign each element — e.g. roast 가지 (eggplant) hot for browning (heat), finish with olive oil (fat), 된장 or anchovy for salt + umami, and rice vinegar or lemon for acid. Example: a bland soup is most often under-salted or under-acidified, not under-spiced — a fix of salt then a few drops of acid will usually transform it before any other ingredient.

## Pitfalls / Fixes
- Over-salting is hard to reverse: add salt incrementally and taste; dilute with unsalted liquid/starch or bulk if overshot
- Adding acid too early can dull it (volatile, and can toughen some proteins) — add most acid at the end
- "Needs more flavor" is usually salt or acid, not more herbs/spices — check those two first
- Pale, steamed-looking food = insufficient heat or a crowded pan trapping moisture; raise heat, dry the surface, work in batches
