# Sweeteners
Sweeteners do far more than make food sweet — they brown, tenderize, retain moisture, balance acid and bitterness, and build body. Knowing how sucrose, fructose, honey, and 조청/물엿 differ in browning speed and hygroscopy lets you swap them creatively and control color, chew, and balance in any dish.

## Behavior / Mechanism
- [fact] Caramelization onset varies by sugar: fructose ~110°C, sucrose/glucose/galactose ~160°C, maltose ~180°C — so high-fructose sweeteners (honey, agave, invert) brown faster and darker #caramelization
- [fact] Two browning paths: caramelization (sugar alone breaking down) and Maillard (reducing sugars + amino acids) — reducing sugars (glucose, fructose, those in honey/조청/물엿) drive Maillard; pure sucrose is non-reducing and must invert first #browning
- [fact] Invert sugar (glucose + fructose, e.g. honey, 물엿/corn syrup, 조청) is hygroscopic — holds water, keeps baked goods moist and soft, resists crystallization, and browns faster than table sugar #hygroscopy
- [technique] Sugar raises the temperature at which proteins coagulate and starch gelatinizes (custards, batters set slower/softer) and competes for water, slowing gluten development → tenderness #structure
- [fact] Sugar balances acid and tames bitterness on the palate and lowers freezing point (softer ice cream, scoopable sorbet) #balance
- [fact] 물엿 (clear corn/rice malt syrup) adds gloss and prevents crystallization in 조림/glazes; 조청 (traditional grain malt syrup) is darker, maltier, less sweet, with deeper flavor #korean

## Selection
- [fact] Granulated sucrose = neutral clean sweetness and predictable caramel; brown sugar adds molasses (moisture + acidity + flavor); honey adds floral aroma + fast browning #selection
- [fact] For Korean glazes/조림: 물엿 for shine and non-crystallizing texture, 조청 for richer color and malt depth, 올리고당 for lighter sweetness #korean
- [fact] Liquid sweeteners are ~25% water — reduce other liquid when substituting them for granulated sugar #substitution-math

## Creative Application & Substitution
Substitute by browning and moisture goals, not 1:1 sweetness: swap up to half the sugar in cookies for honey or invert syrup to get chewier, darker, longer-keeping results — but cut other liquid and lower oven temp since they brown faster. A pinch of sugar balances an over-acidic tomato sauce or a too-sharp vinaigrette. Use 조청 or 물엿 in a Western glaze to add gloss and complexity, or honey + a little soy for a fast Maillard-rich pan glaze on chicken.

## Pitfalls / Fixes
- [fact] Honey/fructose baked goods burning on top before done = brown faster; lower oven temp ~15°C and/or tent with foil #fix
- [fact] Crystallized/grainy caramel: a few drops of acid (lemon) or a spoon of glucose/물엿 inverts sugar and prevents seizing #fix
- [technique] Over-sweet dish: balance with acid, salt, or bitterness rather than diluting #fix
