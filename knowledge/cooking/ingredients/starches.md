# Starches
Rice, potato, flour, and noodles are all starch-delivery systems, and one process — gelatinization — explains how rice goes fluffy, how a sauce thickens, and why a stir-fry sauce turns glossy. Knowing each starch's gelatinization window and amylose/amylopectin balance lets you thicken predictably and swap one starch for another with the right texture.

## Behavior / Mechanism
- [fact] Gelatinization: starch granules absorb water + heat, swell, and burst, releasing amylose/amylopectin that thicken liquid. Onset temps: wheat ~58-64°C, potato ~58-68°C, corn ~62-72°C (full ~95°C), rice ~68-77°C #gelatinization
- [fact] Amylose (linear) sets firm gels and clouds; amylopectin (branched) gives sticky, clear, glossy texture. High-amylose = firm/separate rice; high-amylopectin (찹쌀/glutinous) = sticky/chewy #amylose
- [fact] Cornstarch thickens clear and glossy and ~2x as powerful as flour (use ~½ the cornstarch for the same thickening); potato starch is even clearer and tolerates higher heat #thickening
- [technique] Make a cold slurry (starch + cold water) before adding to hot liquid so granules disperse and don't clump; cook briefly after to lose raw-starch taste #slurry
- [fact] Overcooking/over-stirring pure starch sauces breaks them down and thins them ("gummy then runny"); flour-thickened (roux) sauces are more stable to long cooking and reheating #stability
- [fact] Retrogradation: cooled cooked starch (rice, bread, potato) recrystallizes and goes firm/stale; reheating reverses it. Cornstarch sauces weep and turn runny after freezing #retrogradation
- [fact] Fat (roux) and sugar coat granules and raise gelatinization temperature, slowing thickening — why roux must cook and sweet pie fillings need more starch #interactions

## Selection
- [fact] Choose rice by amylose: long-grain (high amylose) = fluffy/separate; short-grain/찹쌀 = sticky/chewy; rinse to remove surface starch for less gummy rice #rice
- [fact] Choose potatoes by starch: high-starch (russet) = fluffy mash/crisp fry; waxy (red/new) = holds shape in salads/braises #potato
- [fact] Cornstarch/potato starch for clear glossy Asian sauces and gluten-free thickening; flour (roux) for opaque, stable, longer-cooked sauces (gravy, béchamel) #selection

## Creative Application & Substitution
Substitute thickeners by clarity and stability needs: cornstarch ↔ potato starch ~1:1 (both clear, glossy, gluten-free), but use ~½ the amount when replacing flour. For a stir-fry's signature gloss, a cornstarch slurry added at the end coats every piece; for a stew that simmers and reheats, use a roux instead so it won't break. Use the retrogradation trick deliberately — day-old rice is drier and firmer, making it the best base for 볶음밥 (fried rice).

## Pitfalls / Fixes
- [fact] Lumpy sauce = starch added dry to hot liquid; always slurry in cold liquid first, or whisk hard and strain #fix
- [fact] Thin/runny cornstarch sauce after long simmer = overcooked starch; re-thicken with a fresh small slurry off a rolling boil #fix
- [fact] Gummy rice = too much surface starch or stirring; rinse before cooking and don't stir while cooking #fix
- [fact] Sauce thinned after freezing = cornstarch retrogradation; thicken fresh or use a freeze-stable starch (waxy/modified) #fix
