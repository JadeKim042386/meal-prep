# Dairy
Milk, cream, butter, and cheese are fat-protein-water systems, and the fat percentage is the single most useful number for predicting how they behave: it governs whether a sauce stays smooth or curdles, whether it whips, and how richly it browns. Reading dairy by its fat content lets you substitute confidently and rescue a broken sauce on the fly.

## Behavior / Mechanism
- [fact] Casein proteins clump (curdle) when pH drops (acid), at high heat, or with tannins/salt; whey proteins coagulate when heated to ~90°C #curdling
- [fact] Higher fat resists curdling: at ~25%+ fat, fat globules surround denatured casein and block it from forming curds — why heavy cream takes acid and boiling that would split milk (McGee) #fat-percentage
- [fact] Approx fat: whole milk ~3.5%, half-and-half ~10-12%, heavy/whipping cream ~36%, butter ~80% fat / ~16% water / ~4% milk solids #numbers
- [technique] Butter browns (beurre noisette) because its ~4% milk solids undergo Maillard once the water boils off — nutty aroma signals it's done; remove before solids burn black #browning
- [fact] Butter's water content makes it smoke low (~150°C / 302°F); clarifying to ghee removes water + solids, pushing smoke point to ~250°C / 482°F #smoke-point
- [technique] Stabilize milk-based sauces against curdling with a starch (roux/cornstarch) which shields casein, and add acid off-heat or after thickening #stabilize
- [fact] Cream whips because cold fat globules trap air; warm cream won't whip. More fat = more stable foam #whipping

## Selection
- [fact] Match fat to job: heavy cream (~36%) for reduction sauces and whipping; whole milk for béchamel; cultured/full-fat for richer flavor #selection
- [fact] Aged hard cheeses (parmesan) are concentrated umami/glutamate and melt into sauces smoothly; fresh acid-set cheeses (ricotta, paneer, 두부-like) don't melt — they hold shape #cheese
- [fact] European-style butter (~82-85% fat) browns and laminates better than standard (~80%); cultured butter adds tang #butter

## Creative Application & Substitution
Use fat content as your substitution math: thin heavy cream with milk to hit a target richness, or sub coconut cream for dairy cream in a curry (similar fat, won't curdle from acid). Brown butter is a flavor multiplier — drizzle over pasta, fish, vegetables, or even cookies for instant nutty depth. A spoon of cold butter swirled off-heat (monter au beurre) glosses and enriches any pan sauce via emulsification.

## Pitfalls / Fixes
- [fact] Curdled cream sauce: pull off heat, whisk in a splash of cold cream or a cornstarch slurry; strain if needed #fix
- [technique] To add lemon/wine to a cream sauce without splitting, reduce the acid first or use high-fat cream and finish off-heat #fix
- [fact] Grainy cheese sauce = overheated/too-low-fat cheese; use a starch-stabilized base (sodium citrate for ultra-smooth) and keep gentle #fix
