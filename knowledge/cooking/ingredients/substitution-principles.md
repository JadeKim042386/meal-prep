# Substitution Principles
The secret to substituting (and to improvising in general) is to stop thinking about *ingredients* and start thinking about *the job an ingredient does*. Lemon juice isn't "lemon" — it's an **acid** brightening the dish, and any acid can fill that slot. Once you can name the functional role an ingredient plays — salt, acid, fat, umami, aromatic, sweet, heat, body/texture — you can swap freely, knowing exactly what flavor and what structure you must replace. This is the engine of creative cooking: a recipe becomes a set of roles you can recast.

## Behavior / Role
- [technique] SALT role (amplifies all flavor): table/kosher/sea salt, soy sauce, fish sauce, miso/doenjang, anchovy, capers, olives — swap one salty thing for another, then re-taste, because each also carries umami/acid baggage #salt-role
- [technique] ACID role (brightens, balances richness): lemon/lime/vinegar/wine/yogurt/tamarind/sumac/tomato — swap by intensity, and remember liquid acids add moisture while dry ones (sumac, citric acid) don't #acid-role
- [technique] FAT role (carries flavor, builds texture/mouthfeel): butter/oil/cream/coconut milk/cheese/nuts/avocado — match smoke point for high heat and richness level for body; fat is the *flavor carrier*, so swapping it changes how aromas land #fat-role
- [technique] UMAMI role (savory depth): glutamate (kombu, tomato, parmesan, MSG, soy) + nucleotides (mushroom, anchovy, meat) — replace meatiness by stacking a glutamate source with a nucleotide source #umami-role
- [technique] AROMATIC role (top-note identity): herbs, spices, alliums, citrus zest, ginger — swap within the same aroma family (woody↔woody, green↔green) or you change the dish's cuisine #aromatic-role
- [technique] SWEET role (rounds, balances acid/heat): sugar, honey, maple, mirin, fruit, caramelized onion — swap by sweetness power and note that liquids/honey add moisture and brown faster #sweet-role
- [technique] BODY/TEXTURE role (thickening, structure, crunch): flour/cornstarch/roux/egg/reduction (thickening); breadcrumbs/nuts/fried allium (crunch) — these are often invisible until missing #texture-role

## Selection / Handling
- [fact] Before swapping, diagnose: ask "what is THIS ingredient doing here — flavor, structure, or both?" Many ingredients do double duty (lemon = acid + aroma; bacon = fat + salt + umami + smoke) and you must replace ALL the jobs #diagnose
- [fact] Match intensity, not just category — fish sauce is far saltier and funkier than a pinch of salt; start with less and build #intensity
- [fact] Account for hidden moisture and pH: swapping yogurt for cream adds acid and can curdle; swapping honey for sugar adds water and browns faster #side-effects
- [technique] Substitute one variable at a time and taste, so you can tell whether the swap worked #method

## Creative Application & Substitution
Reverse-engineer any dish into a role list, then recast roles to invent a variation: a vinaigrette is fat + acid + salt + aromatic, so "oil + lemon + salt + garlic" can become "sesame oil + rice vinegar + soy + ginger" — same structure, new cuisine. Out of an ingredient? Fill its role: no lemon for fish → any acid (vinegar, a splash of wine, even tomato); no fresh chile → cayenne/gochugaru fills the heat slot; no parmesan → another aged-glutamate source (miso, nutritional yeast) plus salt. Use role-thinking to upgrade, not just patch: if a soup is "flat," it's usually missing a role — add acid for brightness or an umami booster for depth rather than blindly adding more salt.

## Pitfalls / Fixes
- Swap "tastes off" → you replaced flavor but forgot a second job (e.g., subbed lime for the acid but lost the salt that came with the fish sauce).
- Sauce won't thicken after swapping → you removed a structural ingredient (egg, reduction, starch); restore the body role separately.
- Dish too thin/watery after a swap → the replacement carried more moisture (yogurt, honey, fresh vs dried); reduce or adjust.
- "More salt" doesn't fix flatness → the missing role is acid or umami, not salt; taste for which dimension is absent.
