# Umami Boosters
Umami boosters are ingredients dense in the savory amino acid glutamate and the nucleotides inosinate (IMP) and guanylate (GMP) — the molecules your T1R1/T1R3 receptor reads as "savory, satisfying, meaty." The creative key is **synergy**: glutamate alone is good, but glutamate + a nucleotide is up to ~8× stronger, which is why dashi (kombu + bonito) tastes so much deeper than either alone. Keeping a few of these on hand lets you add savory depth to almost anything without more salt.

## Behavior / Role
- [fact] Two booster families: GLUTAMATE sources (kombu, tomato, parmesan, MSG, nutritional yeast, soy) and NUCLEOTIDE sources — inosinate from meat/fish (가다랑어포, anchovy) and guanylate from dried mushrooms (표고) #two-families
- [fact] Synergy: a glutamate + 5'-inosinate mix tastes ~8× stronger than glutamate alone; pairing glutamate with nucleotides is the single biggest umami lever in cooking #synergy
- [fact] 다시마 (kombu): one of the highest natural glutamate sources, ~1,500–3,000 mg/100g — the glutamate half of dashi #kombu
- [fact] 가다랑어포 (katsuobushi/bonito): inosinate ~470–700 mg/100g (glutamate only ~30–40) — the nucleotide half of dashi #katsuobushi
- [fact] 표고 (dried shiitake): ~1,060 mg glutamate AND ~150 mg guanylate/100g; drying creates the guanylate (fresh shiitake has ~70 mg glutamate, little nucleotide) — a vegan way to get BOTH halves #shiitake
- [fact] Parmesan ~1,200–1,680 mg glutamate/100g (long aging frees it); ripe/dried tomato concentrates glutamate (dried 650–1,140 mg/100g); anchovy ~630 mg glutamate/100g #cheese-tomato-anchovy
- [fact] MSG is pure free glutamate (one form of glutamate, identical to that in tomato/cheese) — a clean, dose-controllable umami hit; nutritional yeast is a vegan glutamate-rich powder #msg-nooch
- [pairing] Classic synergy combos: dashi (kombu+bonito), Bolognese (tomato+meat), Caesar (parmesan+anchovy), 된장찌개 (doenjang+anchovy/beef) #pairing

## Selection / Handling
- [fact] 다시마: choose thick, dark pieces with white surface bloom (mannitol = sweetness/umami, not mold); wipe, don't wash off the bloom #selection
- [technique] Make kombu dashi by steeping in cold-then-just-below-boil water (boiling kombu turns it slimy/bitter); add bonito off heat, steep, strain #technique
- [technique] Rehydrate 표고 in cold water slowly for max guanylate; keep the soaking liquid — it's concentrated umami stock #shiitake
- [fact] Use MSG at ~0.1–0.8% of dish weight (a small pinch); too much tastes harsh/metallic, the right amount just tastes "more savory" #dosing
- [fact] Store kombu and dried shiitake airtight and dry; both keep for months to years #storage

## Creative Application & Substitution
The pro move is to **stack a glutamate source with a nucleotide source** in any savory dish: add a parmesan rind (glutamate) to a meat (inosinate) ragù; drop kombu into a chicken stock; fold rehydrated shiitake (both) into a vegetarian braise to mimic meatiness. For a fast flavor "save," stir in a teaspoon of fish sauce, a grating of parm, a spoon of tomato paste, or a pinch of MSG. Substitution by role — to replace meaty depth in a vegan dish, combine kombu/tomato/soy (glutamate) with dried shiitake or nutritional yeast for the missing savory roundness. To dial umami without salt, lean on kombu, mushroom, and MSG (lower-sodium per umami unit than soy/fish sauce).

## Pitfalls / Fixes
- Dish tastes "meaty but flat" → you have glutamate but no nucleotide (or vice versa); add the missing half.
- Broth bitter/slimy → kombu was boiled; steep gently and remove before a hard boil.
- Overdid MSG → tastes metallic; can't remove it, dilute and rebalance with acid/sweetness.
- Stew tastes thin → add tomato paste, a parm rind, dried mushroom, or a splash of soy/fish sauce for instant body.
