# Proteins
Meat, poultry, fish, egg, 두부, and legumes all cook by the same physics — heat denatures proteins, they unfold and bond, and water gets squeezed out — but the temperatures and tipping points differ enough that knowing them is the difference between juicy and dry. Mastering doneness windows and the collagen-to-gelatin transition lets you cook any protein to a target texture and substitute one for another by matching how it behaves under heat.

## Behavior / Mechanism
- [fact] Myosin denatures ~50°C (start of firming); collagen converts toward gelatin ~60-70°C; actin denatures ~66-73°C (150-163°F) and squeezes out moisture, so optimal juicy texture sits ~54-63°C / 130-145°F before actin contracts hard #denaturation
- [fact] Moisture loss in meat rises sharply once internal temp passes ~66°C / 150°F (Kenji) — the case for medium-rare and resting #moisture
- [fact] Tough cuts need 71-93°C / 160-200°F held over time to melt collagen into gelatin, which holds ~10x its weight in water and reads as "moist" despite high temp #collagen
- [fact] Fish/shellfish proteins coagulate lower, ~50-55°C / 120-130°F, so they cook fast and overcook easily #fish
- [fact] Egg whites set ~60-65°C, yolks ~65-70°C — exploited by 63-65°C onsen tamago (just-set white, runny-jammy yolk); above ~66°C whites turn rubbery fast #egg
- [technique] Salt/brine before cooking dissolves myosin and helps proteins retain water (juicier result); dry surface + high heat drives Maillard browning #salt
- [fact] 두부 is set soy protein: nigari (MgCl2) or gypsum (CaSO4) neutralize the proteins' like-charges so they gel; nigari gives softer, sweeter curd, gypsum firmer #tofu
- [fact] Legumes need soaking + simmering to gelatinize starch and break down anti-nutrients; salt early is fine, but acid added early keeps skins tough — add acid after beans soften #legumes

## Selection
- [fact] Marbling (intramuscular fat) = flavor + perceived juiciness; bright color and firm, springy texture signal freshness in meat #quality
- [fact] Fresh fish smells of clean sea not "fishy" (ammonia = old); clear bulging eyes, bright red gills, firm flesh #fish
- [fact] For 두부: 순두부/silken for spoon texture and sauces, 부침/firm for searing and stir-fry; press firm tofu to expel water for better browning #tofu

## Creative Application & Substitution
Substitute proteins by matching the cooking logic, not just the slot: swap a quick-searing fish fillet for chicken breast (both want gentle, brief heat), or a braising beef chuck for pork shoulder (both reward long low collagen melt). For plant swaps, pressed firm 두부 or cooked legumes can stand in for ground meat in tacos or ragù if you replace meat's umami with soy sauce, mushrooms, or miso. A 63°C egg turns a simple bowl of rice or 비빔밥 into a rich, sauce-like dish.

## Pitfalls / Fixes
- [fact] Dry chicken breast = cooked past ~74°C with no carryover buffer; pull at 71-72°C and rest #fix
- [technique] Rest meat after cooking so juices redistribute; carryover adds ~3-5°C #technique
- [fact] Rubbery scrambled eggs = too hot/too long; cook low, pull while still glossy #fix
- [fact] Tough beans even after long cooking = added acid/salt too early or hard water — soften first, season after #fix
