# Fats and Oils
Fat is heat-transfer medium, flavor carrier, and texture-builder all at once, so choosing one means weighing two things: how hot you'll push it (smoke point) and how much you want it to taste like itself (flavor). Knowing which oils are neutral and stable versus which are flavorful and fragile lets you swap freely and never burn a delicate oil on high heat again.

## Behavior / Mechanism
- [fact] Smoke point = where an oil breaks down into smoke, acrid flavors, and free radicals. Refining raises it by removing impurities/free fatty acids #smoke-point
- [fact] Saturated fats (butter, coconut, lard) are most heat-stable; monounsaturated (olive, avocado, peanut) are stable for high heat; polyunsaturated (sunflower, grapeseed, flax) are least stable and oxidize/go rancid fastest #stability
- [fact] Smoke points (refined unless noted): avocado ~270°C/520°F, ghee ~250°C/482°F, refined peanut ~232°C/450°F, refined sunflower ~227°C/441°F, grapeseed ~216°C/421°F, refined canola ~204°C/400°F, refined coconut ~204°C/400°F, light/refined olive ~199-243°C/390-470°F, EVOO ~190°C/374°F, lard ~190°C/374°F, butter ~150°C/302°F #numbers
- [fact] Smoke point drops with use/age as oil breaks into free fatty acids — repeatedly fried oil smokes lower #degradation
- [technique] Fat carries fat-soluble flavor/aroma compounds (chili, garlic, herbs) — blooming spices in oil disperses flavor through a whole dish #flavor-carry
- [fact] Fat coats starch/protein and shortens gluten (hence "shortening"), giving tenderness and richness #texture

## Selection
- [fact] Pick neutral high-smoke-point oils (refined avocado, canola, peanut, grapeseed) for searing, deep-frying, stir-fry; reserve EVOO and toasted sesame for finishing/low heat where their flavor matters #selection
- [fact] EVOO's flavor and antioxidants degrade with high heat — it's fine for everyday sautéing but wasted (and harsh) in deep-frying; use it raw or for gentle cooking to taste it #olive
- [fact] Toasted 참기름 (sesame oil) is a finishing oil — its flavor and low smoke point mean add at the end, not for frying #sesame

## Creative Application & Substitution
Treat oil as a flavor decision: a dish "finished" with toasted sesame, chili oil, or good EVOO tastes completely different from the same dish made with neutral oil, even with identical ingredients. Substitute by matching heat tolerance and flavor profile — swap canola for grapeseed freely (both neutral, high heat), but don't replace finishing EVOO with neutral oil and expect the same brightness. Brown butter or rendered animal fat (lard, schmaltz, bacon fat) can replace plain oil to add deep savory flavor to roasts and sautés.

## Pitfalls / Fixes
- [fact] Bitter/acrid fried food = oil past its smoke point; lower heat or switch to a higher-smoke-point oil #fix
- [fact] Rancid, paint-like smell = oxidized polyunsaturated oil; store oils cool, dark, sealed, and buy small #storage
- [technique] EVOO going bitter when sautéed garlic in it is normal at high heat — keep heat moderate or start garlic in neutral oil #fix
