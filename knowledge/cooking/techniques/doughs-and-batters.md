# Doughs and Batters

Doughs and batters are flour suspensions on a spectrum from stiff (bread) to pourable (crêpe), and where you land is governed by three levers: hydration, gluten development, and leavening. Learn to read a formula in baker's percentages and you can predict and design crumb, chew, rise, and tenderness — the difference between a dense brick and an airy loaf, a tough muffin and a tender one.

## Principle / Mechanism
- [fact] Baker's percentage expresses every ingredient relative to flour = 100%; hydration % = water weight ÷ flour weight #bakers-percentage
- [ratio] Hydration ranges: stiff/firm 55–65% (bagels, pretzels, pasta), medium 60–75% (sandwich bread, rolls), high 75–100% (ciabatta, baguette, pan de cristal) — wetter = more open, larger holes #hydration
- [fact] Gluten = glutenin (elasticity/strength) + gliadin (extensibility/flow); they cross-link into a viscoelastic network only when flour HYDRATES and gets mechanical work #gluten
- [fact] Salt strengthens and tightens the gluten network and slows yeast — typically ~1.8–2% of flour weight #salt
- [ratio] Yeast leavening: biological; CO₂ from fermentation, slow, builds flavor (hours). ~1–2% instant yeast of flour weight #yeast
- [ratio] Baking powder: ~1 tsp per 1 cup (125 g) flour; it is baking soda + acid + starch, double-acting (reacts wet, then with heat) #baking-powder
- [ratio] Baking soda: ~¼ tsp per 1 cup (125 g) flour; needs an ACID in the batter (buttermilk, yogurt, cocoa, brown sugar, 막걸리) to release CO₂ and avoid soapy taste #baking-soda
- [technique] Steam leavening: water flashing to steam lifts high-hydration, high-heat batters/doughs — choux, popovers, puff pastry, croissant lamination #steam
- [technique] More mixing/kneading = more gluten = more chew; less mixing + fat coating ("shortening") = tender, crumbly #mixing

## Why It Works
Flour proteins are inert until water unlocks them; hydration lets glutenin and gliadin link into a stretchy, gas-trapping film, and kneading aligns and strengthens it. Leavening gases — CO₂ from yeast or chemical reaction, or steam — inflate pockets within that film; the gluten (or, in cakes, the egg/starch matrix) must be elastic enough to stretch but strong enough not to rupture, then heat sets the structure permanently. Hydration tunes everything: more water means a slacker, more extensible dough that yeast and enzymes move through faster, producing a more open crumb. Fat tenderizes by coating flour particles and physically shortening gluten strands — hence "shortening." Sugar and acid soften gluten and brown the crust (Maillard + caramelization).

## Creative Application
Think in baker's percentages to riff freely: hold flour at 100% and slide hydration up for a more open, custardy crumb or down for a dense, chewy one. Example 1: convert a stiff 50% dough to a 75% high-hydration version for a focaccia-style 부추 flatbread with big holes and a crisp base. Example 2: choose your leavening by the flavor and timeline you want — yeast for tangy, complex 막걸리-leavened steamed buns (찐빵) with overnight depth; baking powder for fast, neutral pancakes; whipped egg whites for a soufflé-light texture. Mix minimally for tenderness (muffin method: combine wet and dry just until moistened) or knead hard for chew (bread method) — same ingredients, opposite textures, controlled entirely by gluten development.

## Pitfalls / Fixes
- Tough muffins/cakes: overmixed (too much gluten) — stop as soon as dry streaks vanish. #fix
- Dense bread that won't rise: under-developed gluten, dead yeast, or too little time — verify yeast freshness, knead/proof longer, warmer. #fix
- Soapy/metallic taste: excess baking soda or not enough acid to react it — rebalance soda to acid, or switch to baking powder. #fix
- Flat cookies: too little flour or over-creamed/over-warm butter — chill dough; check leavening freshness. #fix
- Gummy crumb: under-baked or hydration too high for the flour — bake to temp (~96–99 °C interior for bread), or lower hydration. #fix
