# Stocks and Mother Sauces

Stocks are flavored water — the blank canvas of savory cooking — and sauces are concentrated, textured flavor painted onto a dish. The French codified five "mother sauces" from which hundreds of derivatives ("daughter sauces") descend, while Japanese cuisine built an entire tradition on 다시 (dashi). Understanding both systems gives you a template for inventing sauces in any cuisine: a base liquid + a thickener/emulsion + a seasoning/aromatic layer.

## Principle / Mechanism
- [fact] The 5 French mother sauces (Escoffier): Béchamel, Velouté, Espagnole, Sauce Tomate, Hollandaise #mother-sauces
- [fact] Béchamel = milk + white roux (+ onion, nutmeg); derivatives: Mornay (+ cheese), cream sauce #bechamel
- [fact] Velouté = light stock (chicken/fish/veal, un-roasted bones) + blond roux; derivatives: suprême, allemande #veloute
- [fact] Espagnole = brown stock (roasted bones) + brown roux + tomato; reduces into demi-glace #espagnole
- [fact] Sauce Tomate = tomatoes + mirepoix + stock (+ traditionally a little roux); the base of countless tomato sauces #tomate
- [fact] Hollandaise = emulsion of egg yolk + butter + acid (no roux) — the odd one out, an emulsified mother #hollandaise
- [ratio] 다시 (awase dashi): ~10 g 다시마(kombu) + 30 g 가쓰오부시(katsuobushi) per 1 L water — yields a ~1:1 glutamate:inosinate ratio #dashi
- [fact] Glutamate (from 다시마/kombu) + inosinate (from 가쓰오부시/멸치) create umami SYNERGY: combined intensity is ~7–8× either alone #umami-synergy
- [technique] 멸치육수 (anchovy stock), 사골육수 (bone stock), and Chinese 上湯 (superior stock) are the East-Asian parallels to French stocks — same role, different aromatics #asian-stock
- [technique] Stocks: cold-water start, low simmer (never boil — boiling emulsifies fat and clouds it), skim scum, strain; roast bones first for brown stock #stock-method

## Why It Works
A stock extracts gelatin (from collagen in bones/connective tissue), glutamate, and aromatic compounds into water. Gelatin gives body and a coating mouthfeel; glutamate and nucleotides (inosinate from fish/meat, guanylate from dried mushrooms) deliver umami. The umami synergy is real chemistry: glutamate and inosinate bind the same T1R1/T1R3 receptor but inosinate locks glutamate into the binding pocket, multiplying perceived intensity — which is exactly why 다시마 + 가쓰오부시, or tomato + Parmesan + mushroom, taste far deeper than their parts. The mother-sauce framework works because each combines a liquid base with a structure-builder (roux for body, or an emulsion for richness) plus an aromatic signature, so swapping any one element generates a new sauce.

## Creative Application
Treat the mother-sauce logic as a generative grammar: base + thickener/emulsion + accent = a sauce. Example 1: a "Korean velouté" — 멸치육수 thickened with a blond roux, finished with 들기름 and toasted 김 for a savory, nutty cream sauce over noodles. Example 2: stack umami sources for synergy — build a vegetarian 다시 from 다시마 (glutamate) + 표고버섯 dried shiitake (guanylate) to hit the same multiplicative depth as a meat stock without meat. Reduce any stock to a glaze for instant gloss and concentrated flavor; mount it with cold butter (monter au beurre) for a quick emulsified pan sauce — Hollandaise/beurre blanc logic applied to any reduction.

## Pitfalls / Fixes
- Cloudy stock: you boiled it — keep at a bare simmer and skim; strain through fine cloth. #fix
- Bitter 다시: 다시마 was boiled (releases bitterness/iodine) — steep kombu below ~60–70 °C and pull before the boil, then add bonito off-heat. #fix
- Flat sauce: missing umami or acid — add a glutamate source (Parmesan, 간장, tomato paste) and a splash of acid to lift. #fix
- Greasy stock: chill and lift the solidified fat cap, or skim during simmering. #fix
- Pasty mother sauce: roux undercooked (raw-flour taste) or too much roux — cook roux longer and thin with more liquid. #fix
