# Thickening and Gelation

Controlling viscosity and set is what separates a watery braise from a glossy sauce, a runny custard from a sliceable terrine. Thickening (raising viscosity of a liquid) and gelation (forming a solid network that traps water) share the same toolkit — starches, proteins, and hydrocolloids — but behave very differently with heat. Knowing the dose and the trigger lets you build any texture from a light nappe to a firm, mouth-melting gel.

## Principle / Mechanism
- [ratio] Roux: 1:1 flour to fat by weight; ~30–60 g roux thickens ~500 mL liquid to a nappe. Darker roux thickens LESS (heat breaks starch) — a dark roux needs ~30% more for equal body #roux
- [technique] Cornstarch slurry: ~1 Tbsp (8 g) starch per 1 cup (240 mL) liquid; mix with cold liquid first, then add — must reach ~90 °C to gelatinize and clarify #slurry
- [fact] Cornstarch gives a glossy, clear set but breaks down with prolonged heat, acid, or freezing; flour gives an opaque, more acid/heat-stable set #starch
- [technique] Reduction: evaporate water to concentrate dissolved solids and gelatin — thickens AND deepens flavor with no added thickener #reduction
- [ratio] Gelatin: bloom in cold water ~5× its weight; ~6–10 g (about 3–5 bronze/silver sheets) sets ~500 mL liquid to a soft jelly. Sets ~15 °C, melts ~30–35 °C — it melts in the mouth #gelatin
- [fact] Gelatin bloom strength (e.g. 140 = bronze, 200 = silver, 250 = gold) measures firmness per gram; higher Bloom = less needed #bloom
- [ratio] Agar: 0.5–2% by weight of liquid (start ~1%); must BOIL to dissolve, sets at ~40–45 °C, stays solid until ~80 °C — heat-stable, vegan, firm/brittle #agar
- [ratio] Pectin: needs sugar (~60%+) and acid (pH ~3.0) to set (HM pectin); LM pectin sets with calcium instead — basis of jams #pectin
- [ratio] Xanthan gum: 0.1–0.3% thickens dressings/sauces; 0.4–0.6% for thick gravies/fillings. Cold-soluble, freeze/acid/heat-stable, suspends particles #xanthan

## Why It Works
Starch granules absorb water and swell when heated past their gelatinization temperature (~60–80 °C); the amylose and amylopectin chains leach out and entangle, trapping water and raising viscosity. Gelatin is partially hydrolyzed collagen: on cooling, the strands re-associate into a triple-helix network that cages water — and because those bonds are weak, gentle mouth heat melts them, giving gelatin's signature melt. Agar is a polysaccharide from seaweed whose chains form a rigid, double-helix network on cooling; the bonds are far stronger, so it sets firmer and resists melting until much hotter (this is "thermal hysteresis" — it sets at 40 °C but melts at 80 °C). Xanthan is a bacterial polysaccharide that forms a weak, shear-thinning network: thick at rest, thin when you pour or whisk, then thick again — ideal for cling without gumminess.

## Creative Application
Pick the texture, then pick the tool. Want a sauce that clings to noodles but pours cleanly? Xanthan at 0.2%. Want a clear, jiggly 다시 jelly garnish that holds at room temp on a hot plate? Agar at ~1%. Want a savory panna-cotta-style 들깨 custard that melts on the tongue? Gelatin. Example 1: a "fluid gel" — set a purée with agar (~1%), then blend the firm gel smooth to get a glossy, pourable, intensely flavored sauce that stays put on the plate. Example 2: spherify a juice with sodium alginate + calcium, or simply reduce a stock with its own gelatin to a sticky glaze (demi-glace logic) for restaurant gloss with zero additives.

## Pitfalls / Fixes
- Lumpy roux or slurry: never add dry starch to hot liquid — make a slurry/roux first, or sift roux in while whisking. #fix
- Gelatin won't set: you boiled it (high heat degrades gelatin) or used too much acid/fresh pineapple/kiwi (enzymes digest the protein) — bloom gently, add fruit cooked. #fix
- Agar grainy or weak: you didn't fully boil it — agar MUST hit a rolling boil to dissolve. #fix
- Xanthan clumps ("fish eyes"): disperse into oil or sugar first, or shower it in slowly while blending. #fix
- Pectin jam won't gel: too little acid or sugar — add lemon juice and verify sugar level, or use LM pectin + calcium. #fix
