# Knife Skills and Cuts

How you cut an ingredient is a cooking decision, not just a prep chore. Cut size and shape control surface area, cooking speed, evenness, mouthfeel, and how flavor is released and absorbed. Uniform cuts cook uniformly; mixed sizes give some pieces that are raw while others are mush. Mastering a handful of standard cuts gives you precise, repeatable control over texture and timing.

## Principle / Mechanism
- [fact] Brunoise: ~3 mm (⅛") cubes — finest dice, made by cross-cutting a julienne #brunoise
- [fact] Julienne ("matchstick"): ~3 mm × 3 mm × 30–50 mm (⅛" × ⅛" × 1–2") #julienne
- [fact] Batonnet ("stick"): ~6 mm × 6 mm × 6 cm (¼" × ¼" × 2½") — the base for small dice #batonnet
- [fact] Dice: small ~6 mm (¼"), medium ~12 mm (½"), large ~18–25 mm (¾"–1") #dice
- [fact] Chiffonade: leafy herbs/greens rolled and sliced into ~4–10 mm ribbons #chiffonade
- [technique] Smaller/uniform cuts = more surface area = faster cooking, faster flavor release/absorption, more even doneness #surface-area
- [technique] Larger cuts = slower cooking, more intact texture, better for long roasts/braises where you want pieces to hold shape #large-cuts
- [technique] Match cut shape to the dish: fine dice for salsa/relish, julienne for quick stir-fry/raw 채, batonnet for fries, large for roasting #matching
- [technique] A sharp knife is safer and cleaner — it requires less force, slips less, and bruises delicate produce less (cleaner cells = less oxidation/weeping) #sharp

## Why It Works
Cooking is heat and mass transfer, and both scale with surface-area-to-volume ratio. Halving a cube's edge length roughly doubles its surface area per unit volume, so heat penetrates faster and the piece cooks quicker — which is why a fine brunoise of carrot softens in a fraction of the time a large dice needs. Uniformity matters because cooking time is set by the largest piece: if cuts vary, you must overcook the small ones to finish the big ones. Surface area also governs flavor: more cut surface means more exposed cells releasing aromatics (a finely minced 마늘 is far more pungent than a smashed clove, because cutting ruptures cells and triggers enzymatic flavor formation) and more area to absorb marinade or seasoning. Shape changes mouthfeel too — the same onion is sharp and crunchy julienned raw, sweet and soft when finely diced and slow-cooked.

## Creative Application
Use the knife as a flavor and texture dial. Example 1: control 마늘's intensity by cut — a whole clove infuses gently, sliced is moderate, microplaned/minced is sharp and pungent (more ruptured cells = more allicin); choose the cut to set the dish's garlic level without changing the amount. Example 2: build textural contrast in one dish — fine brunoise of 당근·애호박 that melts into a sauce, plus batonnet of the same vegetables that stay crisp-tender for bite. For even stir-fries, julienne everything to one size so it cooks in a single fast burst; for a rustic braise, cut large so vegetables hold their shape over the long cook.

## Pitfalls / Fixes
- Uneven cooking: pieces are different sizes — re-cut to uniform dimensions before cooking. #fix
- Bruised, weeping herbs/greens: dull knife or sawing — use a sharp blade and a single clean slice for chiffonade. #fix
- Garlic/onion too harsh: cut finer = more pungent; for milder flavor cut coarser or cook longer. #fix
- Slow, dangerous prep: dull knife forces you to press hard and slip — hone before use, sharpen regularly. #fix
- Vegetables falling apart in a braise: cut too small — go larger so they survive the long cook. #fix
