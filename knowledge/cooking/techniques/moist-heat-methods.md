# Moist Heat Methods

Moist heat cooks through water or water vapor: boiling, simmering, steaming, poaching, braising, stewing, and sous-vide. Because liquid water tops out at 100°C (and steam not much higher at sea level), these methods are gentle and forgiving — ideal for melting tough connective tissue, gently setting delicate proteins, and infusing flavor into the cooking medium itself. Their superpower is precise, low-temperature control of texture.

## Principle / Mechanism
- [fact] Liquid water cooks at ≤100°C; the action is in the lower bands — poaching 70–82°C, simmering 85–96°C, a rolling boil 100°C #temperature
- [technique] Braising = sear then partially submerge in liquid; stewing = fully submerged smaller pieces — both are long, low, collagen-melting methods #braise
- [ratio] Collagen converts to gelatin meaningfully from ~70°C (160°F); the sweet spot is a sustained 71–82°C (160–180°C internal) held for hours #collagen
- [fact] Push past ~95°C internal and muscle fibers over-squeeze and dry into mush even as gelatin liquefies — long-and-gentle beats hot-and-fast #overcook
- [technique] Sous-vide holds a precise water-bath temperature so food cannot exceed the target — doneness becomes a setting, not a guess #sous-vide
- [ratio] Verified sous-vide targets: fish ~50°C, egg ~64°C, medium steak ~58°C (136°F), chicken breast 60°C (silky) to 65°C (firm) #sous-vide-temps

## Why It Works
Water is a far more efficient heat carrier than air, but it is capped near 100°C, so moist heat can never overshoot into browning — that is the trade and the gift. The gentleness lets you target the narrow temperature bands where proteins transform: egg white sets ~62°C, collagen unwinds and hydrolyzes into silky gelatin from ~70°C upward, and that gelatin coats the palate as the lubricated, fall-apart tenderness of a good 갈비찜. Time partly substitutes for temperature: collagen conversion is not instant but accumulates over hours at 70–82°C, which is exactly why tough, gelatin-rich cuts reward patience. Sous-vide takes this to its logical end — set the bath to the exact final temperature and the food self-limits, impossible to overcook on the doneness axis.

## Creative Application
Use the cooking liquid as a second dish. Braise short ribs or 닭볶음탕 and the collagen that leaches out thickens the sauce into a glossy, lip-sticking glaze with zero added starch — reduce it hard at the end. Or exploit sous-vide's precision for textures impossible by other means: 63°C eggs with custardy whites and barely-set yolks, or chicken breast at 60°C that stays juicy for cold salads. Poach delicate fish or chicken in an aromatic, fat-enriched broth (a "court bouillon") and the gentle medium seasons from within while the fat carries aroma deep into the flesh.

## Pitfalls / Fixes
- Tough braise after long cooking → not cooked long enough OR held too cool; keep it a bare simmer (85–95°C liquid) until a fork twists freely.
- Dry, stringy stew meat → boiled too hard/long; drop to a gentle simmer and stop once gelatin sets the texture.
- Bland poached protein → poaching liquid under-seasoned; the food can only be as flavorful as its bath, so salt and aromatize it.
- Sous-vide texture "off" → wrong temperature band; remember texture is the dial — adjust by 1–2°C, not by time.
