# Aroma Compound Families

Since aroma carries most of "flavor," a working map of the major volatile families — what they smell like and which foods make them — is the single most useful creative tool in this whole domain. Group foods by their dominant aroma family and you instantly see which ones can bridge into a shared theme, and which are wildcards. This is the chemical vocabulary behind Flavor Pairing Theory.

## Principle / Mechanism
- [fact] Pyrazines — roasted, nutty, toasted, cocoa, earthy, popcorn; in coffee, cocoa, seared/roasted meat (Maillard), roasted nuts, bread crust, popcorn, bell pepper (raw, green note) #aroma #maillard
- [fact] Esters — fruity, sweet, floral; isoamyl acetate = banana, ethyl hexanoate = apple/pineapple; in ripe fruit, wine, beer, fermented foods #aroma
- [fact] Terpenes — citrus, pine, floral, herbal, resinous; limonene = citrus peel, linalool = floral/lavender, pinene = pine; in citrus, herbs (basil, thyme, mint), hops, cardamom, conifers #aroma
- [fact] Thiols / sulfur compounds — at low levels tropical/savory/garlicky, at high levels rotten/cabbagey; in 마늘·양파 (alliums), brassicas, coffee, passionfruit, roasted meat, Sauvignon Blanc #aroma
- [fact] Lactones — coconut, peach, creamy, buttery; γ-decalactone = peach (creamy/coconut at higher dose); in peach, apricot, coconut, dairy/butter, oak-aged spirits #aroma
- [fact] Aldehydes — fresh, green, grassy, fatty, almond, citrus-peel; (E)-2-hexenal = green/leafy, benzaldehyde = almond/cherry; in fresh herbs, cucumber, citrus, nuts, fats #aroma
- [fact] Maillard volatiles — savory-roasted-meaty-caramel; furans, pyrroles, thiophenes, sotolon (maple/curry/burnt-sugar), furaneol (caramel/strawberry); from browning of proteins+sugars #maillard
- [pairing] Foods sharing a dominant family bridge naturally: pyrazine foods (coffee+cocoa+seared beef+nuts) form one theme #pairing

## Why It Works
Each family is a class of molecules with shared structure and therefore shared odor character. Pyrazines are nitrogen heterocycles formed in the Maillard reaction — they are the smell of "browned" and "roasted," which is why coffee, chocolate, toasted bread, and a seared steak all share an underlying note. Esters form when acids meet alcohols (notably during fruit ripening and fermentation) — the universal "fruity-sweet" signature. Terpenes are the plant kingdom's defense/scent molecules — the bright citrus-pine-floral top notes of herbs, citrus, and spices. Thiols are sulfur-bearing and extraordinarily potent (detectable at parts-per-trillion); they swing from delicious (tropical, roasted) to repellent (rotten) with concentration, which is why alliums and brassicas reward careful cooking. Lactones are cyclic esters with a creamy, coconut-peach roundness — the link between stone fruit, dairy, and oak. Aldehydes provide fresh "green" top notes and, when from fat oxidation, fatty/rancid notes. Maillard volatiles are the deep savory-caramel backbone created by heat. Because each family has a recognizable signature, foods that emphasize the same family can be layered into a coherent aroma, while a wildcard from another family adds contrast.

## Creative Application
Build a dish around one dominant family for coherence, then add a contrast note. Pyrazine theme: coffee-rubbed short rib (seared beef + coffee + cocoa nib) brightened with an ester/terpene wildcard (orange zest). Lactone theme: brown-butter (lactones) roasted peaches with coconut and a green-aldehyde contrast (basil or tarragon). Terpene theme: gin-cured salmon (juniper/citrus terpenes) with dill (terpene-aligned) and a thiol contrast (chive/shallot). Use the map for substitution too: out of basil? Another terpene-forward herb (thyme, mint) keeps the aroma family intact.

## Pitfalls / Fixes
- Thiol overload: overcooked brassicas/garlic tip into sulfurous "rotten" territory — cook gently or briefly.
- Volatile loss: terpenes, esters, and fresh aldehydes are fragile — add citrus zest, fresh herbs, and delicate spices at the END.
- Stacking too many families → muddy, unfocused aroma; pick a lead family and limit contrasts to one or two.
