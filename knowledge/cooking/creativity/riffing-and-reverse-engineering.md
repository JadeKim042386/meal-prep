# Riffing and Reverse Engineering

The fastest way to invent is to steal a working structure and vary it deliberately. **Reverse-engineering** means deconstructing an existing dish into its underlying grammar — its ratio, its slot structure, its technique, its flavor logic — so you understand *why* it works. **Riffing** then means changing exactly one axis at a time (the fat, the acid, the protein, the aroma, the technique) to spin off new dishes that inherit the original's correctness. Ruhlman's *Ratio* is the canonical proof: a fixed proportion "is like having a secret decoder ring" — change the ratio and bread dough becomes pasta becomes pie dough; knowing one ratio is "instantly knowing a thousand" recipes.

## Process / Framework
- [technique] Deconstruct a dish into four layers: **(1) ratio/backbone** (proportions of the core components; [[Ratios and Formulas]]), **(2) structure** (the slots it fills; [[Composing a Dish]]), **(3) technique** (the heat/transformation that defines it; [[Maillard and Caramelization]], [[Moist Heat Methods]], etc.), **(4) flavor grammar** (the cuisine's signature ingredient palette). #deconstruct
- [principle] Ruhlman: a ratio is a fixed proportion that frees you from recipes; know the ratio and you can improvise endlessly within it (vinaigrette 3:1 oil:acid; pie dough 3:2:1 flour:fat:water; roux/sauce; bread 5:3 flour:water). #ratio-decoder
- [technique] Vary ONE axis per riff so the result stays coherent and you learn what each change does (this is the same one-variable discipline as [[Recipe Development Process]]). #one-axis
- [technique] The axes you can swap: **protein** (chicken→tofu→shrimp), **acid** (lemon→vinegar→tamarind; [[Acids]]), **fat** (butter→olive oil→coconut; [[Fats and Oils]]), **aromatic base** (mirepoix→soffritto→sofrito→Cajun trinity; [[Alliums]]), **spice/herb** ([[Spices and Chiles]], [[Herbs]]), **technique** (braise→roast→grill), or **grammar** (swap the whole flavor palette to another cuisine). #axes
- [principle] Swapping the *aromatic base* alone re-locates a dish to a new cuisine: the same braise becomes French (mirepoix + thyme + wine), Italian (soffritto + tomato), Cajun (trinity + paprika), or Indian (onion-ginger-garlic + garam masala). #base-swap
- [technique] To go further, swap the whole grammar at once but keep ratio + structure + technique fixed — disciplined cross-cuisine translation, not random fusion (for true blends use [[Flavor Bridging]]). #grammar-swap
- [decision] Keep what makes it work (the ratio, the technique) and change only the expressive layer (flavor/grammar). If a riff fails, you changed a load-bearing axis — revert and change a softer one. #load-bearing

## How To Apply
**Step 1 — Reverse-engineer.** Take a dish you love and fill this card:
- Ratio: ___ (e.g. risotto ≈ 1 part rice : 3–4 parts stock, added gradually)
- Structure/slots: ___ (base, sauce, fat, acid, crunch, aroma — see [[Composing a Dish]])
- Technique: ___ (e.g. toast rice → deglaze → gradual stock + constant stir for starch release)
- Grammar: ___ (Italian: onion/wine/parmesan/butter)

**Step 2 — Riff one axis.** Hold the ratio + technique constant; change one expressive element. Tabulate options before cooking so you riff on purpose, not by accident.

**Worked riff tree — the vinaigrette (3:1 oil:acid, Ruhlman):**
| Axis changed | Result |
|---|---|
| acid: red wine → lemon | bright lemon vinaigrette |
| acid: → rice vinegar + a drop sesame oil | Asian/Korean-leaning dressing |
| add emulsifier (mustard/egg) | creamy emulsified dressing ([[Emulsification]]) |
| fat: olive oil → toasted walnut oil | nutty vinaigrette for bitter greens ([[Bitterness and Balance]]) |
| add aromatic: roasted shallot + honey | sweet-savory shallot vinaigrette |
One ratio, an entire family of sauces.

## Examples
**Example A — Reverse-engineering a stir-fry into a new dish.** Deconstruct: ratio = protein : veg : sauce roughly 2:2:1; structure = sear protein → cook aromatics → veg → bind with sauce → finish; technique = high-heat wok ([[Dry Heat Methods]], [[Heat Management]]); grammar = [[Chinese Flavor Grammar]] (ginger-garlic-scallion + soy + Shaoxing). **Riff:** keep ratio + technique, swap grammar to [[Thai Flavor Grammar]] — aromatic base becomes garlic-chili-galangal, sauce becomes fish sauce + lime + palm sugar + Thai basil ([[Vietnamese Flavor Grammar]] is one more hop away by swapping basil for mint + more lime). Same stir-fry skeleton, a brand-new dish, because only the expressive layer moved.

**Example B — A new soup from a known ratio.** Reverse-engineer a basic pureed vegetable soup: ratio ≈ 1 part aromatic : 4 parts main veg : enough stock to cover, then blend + finish with fat + acid ([[Thickening and Gelation]] via the puree itself). **Riff one axis at a time:** main veg cauliflower → roasted carrot (now sweeter, add cumin); fat cream → coconut milk (now it reads Thai — bridge with lime + curry per [[Flavor Bridging]]); finish acid lemon → lime. Each single swap yields a coherent new soup because the ratio and the blend-and-finish technique are untouched. Salt and balance every version via [[Salt Fat Acid Heat]].
