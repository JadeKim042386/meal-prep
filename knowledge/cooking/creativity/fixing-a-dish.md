# Fixing a Dish

A diagnostic lookup table for rescuing a dish that's off-balance. The governing idea (Nosrat, and confirmed across America's Test Kitchen and other test-kitchen sources) is that the basic tastes counterbalance each other: salt suppresses bitterness and amplifies perceived flavor, acid cuts richness and tames over-salting/over-sweetness, sweetness softens heat and acidity and bitterness, and fat coats the tongue to physically blunt acidity and heat. So most problems are fixed not by removing the offending element (usually impossible) but by **adding its counterweight**. The corrections below are cross-verified against ≥2 authoritative sources; add in small increments and re-taste at serving temperature.

## Process / Framework
- [principle] You can't subtract a taste once it's in — you **rebalance** by adding a counterweight, or dilute/bulk up the whole dish. (Nosrat; America's Test Kitchen.) #counterweight
- [principle] Taste interactions to remember: **acid tames salt, sweet, and bitter**; **sweet tames heat, acid, and bitter**; **salt suppresses bitterness and lifts flatness**; **fat blunts acidity and heat**; **bitterness/heat cut excess sweetness**. #interactions
- [principle] Always add corrections a little at a time, stir, and re-taste — overshooting creates a new problem. Adjust final seasoning just before serving (Nosrat). #increment
- [technique] Two universal escape hatches when no single counterweight works: **dilute** (add unsalted/unseasoned stock or water) or **bulk up** (add more of the unseasoned base — beans, rice, potato, extra veg) to spread the offending element thinner. #dilute-bulk
- [technique] "Missing something" almost always = **needs salt OR needs acid** — try a pinch of salt first, then a few drops of acid; one of them usually unlocks the dish (see [[Seasoning and Salting]], [[Acidity and Brightness]]). #flat-fix

## How To Apply
**Diagnostic lookup table** (verified across America's Test Kitchen + Today/Harvest test-kitchen guidance + Nosrat):

| Problem | Primary fix | Secondary / mechanism | Cross-ref |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Too salty** | Add acid (lemon/lime/vinegar) and/or a little sweetener; add unsalted bulk (rice, potato, beans, unsalted stock); add water if over-reduced | Acid and sweet rebalance perceived salt; bulk/dilution spreads it thinner. (The raw-potato "myth" mostly just adds bulk.) | [[Seasoning and Salting]], [[Acids]] |
| **Flat / bland** | Add salt first; then a few drops of acid; then an umami booster | Salt amplifies flavor; acid lifts the finish; umami adds savory depth | [[Seasoning and Salting]], [[Umami and Synergy]], [[Umami Boosters]], [[Building Depth and Layering]] |
| **Too greasy / fat sits on top** | Skim/blot; add acid to cut; add a starch to emulsify/bind; if a broken emulsion, re-emulsify | Acid cuts grease perception; starch + agitation binds fat into the liquid | [[Emulsification]], [[Fat as Flavor Carrier]], [[Acidity and Brightness]] |
| **Too sweet** | Add acid (vinegar/citrus); add salt; add heat (cayenne) or bitterness; for desserts, espresso powder/liqueur | Acid, salt, bitterness, and heat all counter sweetness and add layers | [[Acidity and Brightness]], [[Bitterness and Balance]], [[Spices and Chiles]] |
| **Too bitter** | Add salt (small) and a little sweetener; add fat; add acid | Salt suppresses bitterness (and not vice-versa); sweet/fat/acid mask it | [[Bitterness and Balance]], [[Seasoning and Salting]], [[Fat as Flavor Carrier]] |
| **Too acidic / sour** | Add fat (butter, cream, cheese, oil); add a sweetener; tiny pinch of baking soda to neutralize | Fat coats tongue and blocks acid receptors; sweet balances; baking soda chemically neutralizes acid | [[Fat as Flavor Carrier]], [[Acidity and Brightness]], [[Fats and Oils]] |
| **Too spicy / hot** | Add fat/dairy (cream, yogurt, coconut milk); add a sweetener; add acid; bulk up | Capsaicin is fat-soluble — dairy/fat carries it away; sweet + acid + dilution reduce intensity | [[Fat as Flavor Carrier]], [[Spices and Chiles]], [[Dairy]] |
| **Too thin / watery** | Reduce by simmering; add a starch slurry or roux; puree/mash some of the solids; add a beurre manié | Evaporation concentrates; starch/protein gels thicken; pureed solids add body | [[Thickening and Gelation]], [[Stocks and Mother Sauces]] |
| **Tastes "muddy" / no top notes** | Finish with fresh herbs, citrus zest, raw allium, or a drizzle of good oil | Volatile aroma compounds lift a dish that's all base and no top (see aroma below) | [[Taste and Aroma Perception]], [[Herbs]], [[Aroma Compound Families]] |
| **Lacks depth / one-dimensional** | Layer umami (soy, miso, parmesan, mushroom, tomato paste, fish sauce); add a Maillard/caramelized element | Builds a savory floor and roasty complexity under the bright notes | [[Building Depth and Layering]], [[Umami and Synergy]], [[Maillard and Caramelization]] |
| **No textural interest** | Add a crunch garnish (toasted nuts/seeds, fried shallot, crouton, crisp skin) | Contrast in [[Texture and Mouthfeel]] is a flavor in its own right | [[Texture and Mouthfeel]], [[Composing a Dish]] |

**Workflow:** (1) Taste and name the single biggest problem. (2) Look it up. (3) Add the primary fix in a small increment. (4) Stir, wait, re-taste. (5) Stop the moment it reads balanced — don't chase perfection into a new imbalance.

## Examples
**Example A — Over-salted soup, 15 min before dinner.** Diagnose: too salty (over-reduced lentil soup). Lookup → add unsalted bulk + acid + dilution. Action: stir in a cup of unsalted stock/water (dilute), add a handful of cooked rice (bulk to absorb), then brighten with a squeeze of lemon (acid rebalances perceived salt). Re-taste: balanced. Note we never "removed" salt — we counterweighted and diluted it. (See [[Seasoning and Salting]], [[Acidity and Brightness]].)

**Example B — A curry that's "good but boring" and too hot.** Two problems, fix in order. (1) Too spicy → stir in coconut milk/yogurt: capsaicin is fat-soluble, so dairy/fat physically carries the heat away ([[Fat as Flavor Carrier]], [[Dairy]]); a pinch of sugar softens the edge. (2) Now flat/boring → add salt first, then a teaspoon of fish sauce or a squeeze of lime for umami + acid, and finish with fresh cilantro for top-note aroma ([[Umami and Synergy]], [[Herbs]], [[Taste and Aroma Perception]]). The dish goes from harsh and dull to round and lively without re-cooking.
