# Recipe Development Process

The repeatable loop for turning an idea into a reliable, delicious recipe: **concept → structure → flavor balance → technique choice → cook → taste → adjust → lock the formula**. It borrows the discipline of the test kitchen (Kenji López-Alt: hypothesis, change one variable, measure) and the intuition of the elements cook (Samin Nosrat: control Salt, Fat, Acid, Heat and you need no recipe). The goal of a first pass is not perfection but *information* — a dish good enough to diagnose. You then iterate, changing exactly one axis per round so you always know what caused the change.

## Process / Framework
- [principle] Start from a concept, not a list: name the dish in one line (cuisine pull, hero ingredient, vibe). Concept constrains every later choice. #concept
- [technique] Lay the structural skeleton first using [[Composing a Dish]]: protein/starch/veg + sauce + fat + acid + crunch + aroma. A complete plate before you tune any single flavor. #structure
- [technique] Set a backbone ratio from [[Ratios and Formulas]] / Ruhlman before measuring loosely (vinaigrette 3:1 oil:acid, brine 1:16 salt:water, roux-thickened sauce). Ratios let you scale and improvise instead of memorizing. #ratio
- [principle] Borrow a coherent flavor logic from a single grammar first (one of the [[Korean Flavor Grammar]] / [[Italian Flavor Grammar]] / [[Thai Flavor Grammar]] etc.) before fusing; fusion is a later, deliberate move via [[Flavor Bridging]]. #grammar
- [technique] Change ONE variable per test (Kenji's core rule): salt level, acid type, cook temp, or one ingredient — never several at once, or you cannot attribute the result. #single-variable
- [technique] Take notes every round: weights in grams, times, temps, and a one-line tasting verdict. A recipe is the *log* of the winning round, not the first attempt. #notes
- [principle] Taste constantly and taste at serving temperature; cold/hot dulls salt and acid perception (see [[Taste and Aroma Perception]]). Adjust seasoning last, just before serving. #tasting
- [decision] Final balance check before locking: is it salted enough ([[Seasoning and Salting]]), is there enough acid to lift it ([[Acidity and Brightness]]), enough fat to carry aroma ([[Fat as Flavor Carrier]]), and a textural contrast ([[Texture and Mouthfeel]])? If any "no", fix per [[Fixing a Dish]]. #balance
- [principle] Lock the formula only after it survives a clean repeat: cook it again from your own notes and confirm it reproduces. #reproducibility

## How To Apply
The loop, step by step:

1. **Concept (1 sentence).** e.g. "Weeknight 고추장-glazed salmon traybake, sweet-savory-spicy, ready in 25 min."
2. **Skeleton.** Pick each slot of [[Composing a Dish]]: protein = 연어; starch = roasted potatoes; veg = broccolini; sauce = 고추장 glaze; fat = sesame oil + the salmon's own fat; acid = rice vinegar / lime; crunch = toasted sesame; aroma = scallion + garlic.
3. **Backbone ratios.** Glaze 2:1:1 고추장:꿀:간장, thinned with a splash of rice vinegar. Salt the salmon at ~1% of its weight (see [[Seasoning and Salting]]).
4. **Technique choice.** High dry heat for Maillard crust and to render fat ([[Dry Heat Methods]], [[Maillard and Caramelization]]); glaze added late so sugars don't scorch ([[Heat Management]]).
5. **Cook round 1, taste, diagnose.** "Tasty but flat and a touch sweet." Diagnosis → needs acid and salt; sweetness too high.
6. **Adjust ONE axis.** Round 2: add lime at the end (acid only). Re-taste → "brighter, sweetness now reads balanced." Round 3 if needed: nudge salt.
7. **Confirm + lock.** Cook from notes once more; if it repeats, write the recipe.

Common failure mode: changing the glaze ratio AND the cook temp AND adding lime in one round — you'll never know which fixed it. Discipline of one-variable testing is what separates development from guessing.

## Examples
**Example A — Inventing a "Gochujang Caesar" dressing.** Concept: Korean-leaning Caesar. Skeleton = emulsified dressing. Backbone ratio borrowed from classic Caesar but reverse-engineered (see [[Riffing and Reverse Engineering]]): fat (oil + egg yolk) : acid (lemon) : umami (anchovy) : pungent (garlic). Swap anchovy-only umami for anchovy + 고추장 (both carry deep umami; see [[Umami and Synergy]], [[Fermented Condiments]]). Test 1: too thick, too spicy. Single-variable fixes across rounds — thin with lemon (acid), then dial 고추장 down by half. Lock at fat:acid:umami ≈ 3:1:1 with 고추장 as a flavor accent. Emulsify per [[Emulsification]].

**Example B — A new white-bean braise.** Concept: creamy Tuscan-style bean bowl with a North African twist. Skeleton + grammar: base on [[Italian Flavor Grammar]] (soffritto, olive oil, parmesan rind for depth via [[Building Depth and Layering]]), then bridge to [[North African Flavor Grammar]] with a spoon of harissa. Backbone: 1 part aromatics : 4 parts beans : enough stock to barely cover. Round 1 reads muddy → add lemon + a parmesan-rind-free hit of acid (one variable). Round 2 reads thin → reduce harder / mash a ladle of beans to thicken ([[Thickening and Gelation]]). Finish with olive oil and herbs ([[Herbs]]) for the aroma slot.
